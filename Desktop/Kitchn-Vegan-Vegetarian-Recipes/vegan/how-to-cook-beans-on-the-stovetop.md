# How to Cook Beans on the Stovetop

**Diet:** vegan

Emma is a former editor for The Kitchn and a graduate of the Cambridge School for Culinary Arts. She is the author of True Brews and Brew Better Beer. Check out her website for more cooking stories

## Ingredients

- 1 pound dried beans, any kind
- Water
- 2 to 3 teaspoons
- salt, plus more as needed
- #### Aromatics (optional):
- Bay leaf, 1 to 2 whole garlic cloves, 1/2 onion, chopped carrots

## Method

1. Spread 1 pound dried beans on a rimmed baking sheet in a single layer. Pick through the beans, removing any shriveled beans or other debris.
2. Transfer the beans to a colander or fine-mesh strainer and rinse until the water runs clear.
3. Place the dried beans in a Dutch oven or other heavy cooking pot. Add the aromatics, if using.
4. Add enough water to cover the beans by about an inch. Bring them to a boil over medium-high heat.
5. Once boiling, reduce the heat to low and a very gentle simmer. You should barely see the water moving. Leave the lid off for firm beans meant for cold salads and pasta dishes. Cover the pot with the lid slightly ajar for creamier beans for soups, casseroles, and burritos. (Learn more: Leaving the Lid On Vs. Off When Cooking Beans.)
6. Cook the beans for 1 hour, and then begin checking for doneness. Depending on their age, size, and variety, beans can take anywhere from 1 to 4 hours to cook through. Be patient. Keep the beans at a gentle simmer and taste frequently as they start to become tender. Add more water as needed to keep the beans submerged, and stir occasionally.
7. When beans are tender but still too firm to enjoy eating, add the salt. Adding the salt too early can keep the beans from becoming tender. Continue simmering until the beans are as tender and creamy as you like them. Taste and season with more salt as needed.
8. If not serving immediately, cool the beans in their cooking liquid. Transfer the beans and cooking liquid to airtight containers and refrigerate or freeze.
