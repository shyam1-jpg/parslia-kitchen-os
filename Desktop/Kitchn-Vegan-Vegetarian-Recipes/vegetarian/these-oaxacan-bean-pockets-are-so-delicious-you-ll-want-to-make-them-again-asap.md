# These Oaxacan Bean Pockets Are So Delicious, You'll Want to Make Them Again ASAP

**Diet:** vegetarian

Title: These Oaxacan Bean Pockets Are So Delicious, You'll Want to Make Them Again ASAP

## Ingredients

- #### For the beans:
- 3 tablespoons olive oil
- 1/2 small white onion, thinly sliced (1 cup)
- 2 cloves
- garlic, smashed and peeled
- 1 dried chile de arbol, trimmed and seeded
- 1 dried avocado leaf (optional but highly recommended)
- 1 (about 15-ounce) can
- black beans (do not drain)
- 1 sprig
- fresh green epazote (see recipe notes)
- 1/4 teaspoon kosher salt, plus more as needed
- #### For topping and serving:
- 4 ounces Cotija cheese, crumbled (1 cup)
- 4 ounces queso fresco cheese, crumbled (1 cup)
- Mexican crema or sour cream thinned out with a little milk or water, for drizzling
- Salsa verde(optional)
- #### For the masa dough:
- 2 cups masa harina, plus more as needed
- 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt
- 2 cups warm water, plus more as needed
- 1 teaspoon olive oil
- #### Make the beans:

## Method

1. Heat 3 tablespoons olive oil in a 10-inch stainless steel or nonstick frying pan over medium-high heat until shimmering. Add 1/2 small thinly sliced white onion, 2 smashed and peeled garlic cloves, 1 trimmed and seeded dried chile de arbol, and 1 pinch kosher salt. Stir to coat everything with oil, and cook until the onion begins to soften, 2 to 3 minutes.
2. Add 1 dried avocado leaf and cook, stirring occasionally, until the onion begins to brown, about 3 minutes. Add 1 (about 15-ounce) can black beans, including the canning liquid. Fill the can halfway with water, swirl the can to loosen any remaining bits of bean, and pour into the pan. Stir in 1 fresh green epazote sprig and 1/4 teaspoon kosher salt.
3. Bring to a boil. Reduce the heat as needed and simmer until the liquid thickens enough that a spatula scraped down the center of the pan leaves a distinct trail that doesn't close up right away, 10 to 12 minutes. Remove the pan from the heat.
4. Remove and discard the epazote, avocado leaf, and chile. Mash the beans with a bean or potato masher or a fork into a paste. Let cool completely (it will thicken as it cools), about 15 minutes. Meanwhile, make the topping and masa dough.
5. Stir 4 ounces crumbled Cotija cheese and 4 ounces queso fresco cheese together in a medium bowl. Refrigerate until ready to use.
6. Mix 2 cups masa harina and 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt together in a large bowl. Add 2 cups warm water in 1/2-cup increments, mixing it in with your hands positioned like a claw and then kneading until you can form smooth and soft balls that you can press your thumb into without them cracking (this is called the thumbprint test). If the dough is too sticky, mix in more masa harina in 1/4-cup increments. If it's too dry, mix in more warm water in 1/4- cup increments.
7. Divide and roll the dough into about 12 golf-ball sized portions (about 60 grams each). Cover with a kitchen towel. Let rest for 5 to 10 minutes while you heat the pan.
8. Brush 1 teaspoon olive oil on a comal, or cast iron griddle, or large cast iron skillet; wipe off the excess with a paper towel. Turn the heat on to medium-high. Meanwhile, assemble the first tetela.
9. Cut two squares out of a plastic produce or zip-top bag roughly the size of a tortilla press (see recipe notes if you don’t have a press). Open the press, place a plastic square on it, and top with one masa ball. Gently press three fingers into the middle of the ball. Cover with the second plastic square and gently press with the tortilla press. If uneven or less than 5-inches wide, flip the masa, still sandwiched between the plastic, and press again until 5-inches wide. Open the tortilla press and remove the top layer of plastic (tap on the plastic to dislodge the dough if needed).
10. Assemble the first tetela: Top the dough with 1 tablespoon of the beans, then gently spread into an even layer, leaving a 1-inch border. Using the bottom sheet of plastic, fold the bottom of the dough round up about a third over the filling. With the plastic still covering it, gently press down on the folded edge. Tap the plastic to dislodge the dough (do this tap at any point in assembly if the dough is sticking to the plastic) and peel the plastic back from that part. Using the same technique, fold another side of the dough at a 45º angle over the filling so you now have a cone shape. Finally, fold the remaining side of the dough over the filling, making sure it is completely covered, so you now have a triangle shape. Gently press the tetela down a little bit to distribute the beans and flatten it in case they're too concentrated in the middle.
11. When the comal is hot, slide a flat spatula under the tetela. Flip the tetela onto the comal so it is folded-side down. Use the spatula to nudge the sides back into straight lines if desired. Cook, reducing or increasing the heat as needed, until the edges are more opaque, look drier, and start to show tiny cracks, 3 to 6 minutes. Meanwhile, assemble the next tetela (do not assemble more than one or they will dry out before you can cook them).
12. Flip the tetela on the comal and cook until the second side is opaque and charred in spots, about 4 minutes more. Transfer to a large plate or wire rack. Repeat cooking and assembling the remaining tetelas, brushing the comal with more oil as needed, until you run out of masa dough or filling.
13. To serve, drizzle the tetelas with Mexican crema, and sprinkle with the cheese mixture. Serve with salsa verde if desired.
