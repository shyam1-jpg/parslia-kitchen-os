# I Make a Pot of This Delicious Quinoa Chili Every Sunday of Fall

**Diet:** vegetarian

Megan is a freelance writer, recipe developer and cookbook writer. Her first book, Whole-Grain Mornings, (Ten Speed Press) is available in bookstores nationwide.

## Ingredients

- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1 medium yellow onion, diced
- 5 cloves
- garlic, minced
- 1 1/2 tablespoons chili powder
- 1 tablespoon ground coriander
- 1 (14.5-ounce) can fire-roasted tomatoes
- 1/2 pound dried black beans, rinsed well
- 1 chipotle chile from canned chipotle chiles in adobo, minced
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano
- 5 cups water
- 1 1/2 cups sweet potatoes (2 to 3 small), cut into 1/2-inch cubes
- 1/4 cup quinoa, rinsed and drained
- 2 teaspoons kosher salt plus more to taste
- Freshly ground black pepper
- Sour cream, for garnish (optional)
- Chopped scallions, for garnish (optional)
- Finely chopped cilantro, for garnish (optional)

## Method

1. Heat 1 tablespoon olive oil in heavy large pot over medium heat. Add 1 diced medium yellow onion and cook until soft and beginning to brown, 6 to 7 minutes. Add 5 minced garlic cloves, 1 1/2 tablespoons chili powder, and 1 tablespoon coriander and stir. Cook together for 1 minute.
2. Stir in 1 (14.5-ounce) can fire-roasted tomatoes with their juices, 1/2 pound dried black beans, 1 minced chipotle pepper, and 1 teaspoon dried oregano. Add 5 cups of water and bring to a boil. Reduce heat to low, cover with lid slightly ajar and simmer until beans are flavorful and tender, anywhere from 2 to 4 hours (depending on the age of your beans).
3. After 1 1/2 hours of cooking, add 1 1/2 cups cut sweet potatoes, 1/4 cup quinoa, and 2 teaspoons kosher salt. Place the pot's lid back on slightly ajar and allow to simmer on low heat until the beans are soft and the sweet potatoes and quinoa are cooked through. Add more water if the chili becomes too thick. Season to taste with kosher salt and black pepper. Serve with sour cream, chopped scallions, and finely chopped cilantro, if using.
