# How to Make the Softest, Creamiest Scrambled Eggs

**Diet:** vegetarian

Emma is a former editor for The Kitchn and a graduate of the Cambridge School for Culinary Arts. She is the author of True Brews and Brew Better Beer. Check out her website for more cooking stories

## Ingredients

- 1 teaspoon unsalted butter
- 2 or more
- large eggs
- 1/4 teaspoon kosher salt
- Freshly ground black pepper
- 1 tablespoon heavy cream or whole milk per egg (optional)
- Chopped fresh herbs (optional)

## Method

1. Place the pan over low heat on your stove. Add 1 teaspoon unsalted butter and let it begin to melt while you whisk the eggs.
2. Crack 2 or more large eggs into a bowl. Whisk them vigorously until the whites and yolks are completely mixed, and the eggs are a bit frothy.
3. Whisk 1/4 teaspoon kosher salt and a few grinds of black pepper into the eggs. If you like, also add a tablespoon of heavy cream or milk. Whisk to combine.
4. Pour the eggs into the pan. The eggs will spread out in a thin layer — that's fine.
5. Stir the eggs occasionally with a spatula. At first the eggs will be very liquidy; then you'll notice large, opaque curds starting to form. Eventually, the eggs will no longer run in to fill the gaps when you stir. Frequent stirring will give you smaller curds; stirring less frequently will give you larger, irregular curds. Continue cooking until the eggs are as soft or as firm as you like them, about 10 to 15 minutes.
6. Remove the eggs from heat slightly _before_ they're done. The eggs will continue cooking for a minute or two off the heat, so remove them when they're almost — but not quite — as cooked as you like them. If you want to add chopped fresh herbs, stir them in now, or sprinkle over the top.
7. Slide the eggs onto a plate and serve.
