## 1. Positive response

Thank you for putting this on paper. The draft is the first document that treats the Kitchen as a technical section with a clear boundary, rather than a place that absorbs every hospitality gap. That is the right starting point, and I welcome it.

I particularly support the following.

**Technical ownership is named.** Food quality, food safety, allergens, menus, production planning, stock requirements and meal readiness sit with the Kitchen. That is how a professional kitchen must work. It also protects Shannon and Louise: they can coordinate people without being asked to take food-safety decisions they should not own.

**The Restaurant boundary is the right one.** Buffet presentation, table clearing, dining-room reset, tea and coffee, guest refreshment fruit and routine restaurant crockery washing are not Kitchen work. Writing that down will stop skilled chef hours being used as a default wash-up and FOH backup.

**Chef relief and succession are named as a requirement.** Asking the Kitchen to develop an In-Charge and a Reliever, so the operation does not depend on one or two people, is correct. I have been carrying too much of the gap myself. A model that plans cover, rather than hoping I will fill every hole, is the professional way forward.

**Protected management time is the right instinct.** Head Chef work is not “whatever is left after service”. Menu, allergen control, ordering, training and a 7-day plan need time inside the ordinary week. The draft understands that.

**One Hospitality team, with a food-safety line that cannot be crossed, is sound.** Kitchen support can help a non-technical bottleneck when Kitchen work is genuinely complete. Restaurant can help with a delivery or a wash-up spike. Neither move should put allergens, hygiene or technical standards at risk. If we cannot agree, the GM decides. That is a clean employment rule.

**The 4-week pilot and “prove the hours before we add headcount” discipline is fair.** I do not want ghost posts. I do want enough cover to run a safe 20-guest kitchen without excessive hours. Measuring service readiness, quality, safety, off-days and extra hours is the right idea.

**The close-to-open rule is correct.** A chef finishing at 21:00 must not be rostered onto the 07:00 shift the next morning. That is a working-time and safety issue, not a preference.

I want this proposal to succeed. For it to succeed as an employment document, the guest number must be the real number, each role must have a full work description, and the gaps in the script must be fixed before week one.

## 2. Required correction: 20 guests, not 30–35

Every staffing, overlap, support and “skilled hours” assumption in the draft is built on a **30–35 guest** baseline.

**The correct working baseline for this kitchen, and for this pilot, is 20 guests.**

That is not a small edit. It changes the model.

| Draft figure | Correct figure | Why it matters |
|--------------|----------------|----------------|
| 30–35 guest pilot | **20 guests** | Prep volume, wash-up, service intensity and chef overlap all scale with covers. |
| Two chef shifts sized for 30–35 | Same clock times may still work, but they must not be staffed as a 35-cover brigade | A 4-hour daily overlap on 20 lunches will waste skilled hours unless it is used for handover and Head Chef planning. |
| Three full-time KA/KP posts as the original model | Confirm actual hours at **20 guests**; do not open as if 35 covers | Support windows still matter. Permanent headcount must follow 20-guest demand, not a 30–35 assumption. |
| Measures written as “30–35 guest pilot standard” | Measures written as **20-guest pilot standard** | Otherwise we will judge the wrong operation and make the wrong headcount decision. |

Please replace **30–35** with **20 guests** wherever the draft uses it: purpose, chef-shift title, pilot standards table, and any later paper that copies this model.

If a retreat week later rises toward 30 guests, that is a **peak variant**, not the baseline. The pilot should measure the normal house — 20 guests — and record any higher week as an exception with a reason.

## 3. Full work description — what I am actually employed to do

The draft lists bullets under “Shyam’s Role”. That is a purpose note, not an employment work description. A work description says what is done every day, what is done every week, what is not done, who the role reports to, and how the role is judged.

The full descriptions for every Kitchen post are in `02-Full-Work-Descriptions-20-Guests.md`. The Head Chef description is summarised here so the response is complete on its own.

### Head Chef — Shyam

**Purpose.** Own the Kitchen technically at a 20-guest baseline: food quality, food safety, menus, production, stock and chef standards. Lead and plan the Kitchen. Do not routinely carry Restaurant work, Duty Manager work, or every operational gap in the building.

**Reports to.** General Manager for employment, performance and final dispute. Works with Shannon and Louise on live deployment. They do not override a genuine food-safety or technical Kitchen requirement.

**Daily work at 20 guests.**

- Confirm guest count, allergens, diet notes and service times for the day.
- Set or confirm the day’s menu, quantities and named dish owners.
- See that breakfast, lunch and dinner are produced to standard and handed to Restaurant on time.
- Check temperatures, labelling, allergen control and production hygiene.
- Oversee Kitchen pot wash, Kitchen cleaning and the technical close for the shift pattern in force.
- Review shortages and the order list; authorise or place Kitchen orders as required that day.
- Brief the chef on duty and the KA/KP support; take a proper handover.
- Record any unplanned extra hour or support move, with the reason.
- Complete the Head Chef planning block when the rota has protected it — menu, 7-day plan, orders, training notes — inside the ordinary shift.

**Weekly work.**

- Publish a 7-day plan: menu, 20-guest quantities, prep owners, stock and orders, chef and KA/KP allocation, allergens, risks and the support requirement for Shannon and Louise.
- Confirm the 48-hour picture: final count, substitutions, deliveries and any targeted support change.
- Hold a short training or standards session with the In-Charge and Reliever.
- Review waste, food-cost movement and recurring bottlenecks.
- Give Shannon and Louise a clear staffing and support requirement for the following week. They coordinate deployment. They do not redesign the Kitchen.

**What this role does not own.**

- Restaurant buffet setup, guest-facing presentation, table clearing or dining-room reset.
- Tea, coffee, refreshment area, guest biscuits, refreshment fruit or FOH milk and alternatives.
- Routine Restaurant crockery and mug washing.
- Reception, check-in, housekeeping or Estates work. Kitchen reports faults; Estates owns the fix.
- The Duty Manager function. Shannon and Louise hold that.

**How the role is judged at 20 guests.** Meals ready for the agreed service time; agreed quality held; no safety compromise; weekly off-days held without repeated excessive hours; 7-day and 48-hour plans usable for staffing and ordering; extra hours recorded; In-Charge and Reliever actually developed, not only named on paper.

## 4. Issues found in the script

The script is well intended. It is not yet safe to issue as an employment or rota document. These are the problems, in detail.

### 4.1 The guest number is wrong, so the whole model is sized for the wrong house

The purpose, the chef-shift heading and the measures table all say **30–35 guests**. This kitchen’s working baseline is **20 guests**.

At 35 covers, a four-hour chef overlap and three KA/KP posts can be argued. At 20 covers, the same structure will look overstaffed on a quiet day and will not tell us the truth about “actual hours required”. Food-cost, prep, pot wash and deep-clean load all change with covers. If we pilot the 35-cover model on a 20-guest house, we will either waste skilled hours or conclude we need people we do not need.

**Fix:** rewrite the paper for **20 guests**. Treat any week near 30 as a recorded peak, not the normal establishment.

### 4.2 “Shyam should not carry every gap” is then contradicted by the same section

Section 1 says I should lead and plan rather than routinely carry every operational gap. The same section then gives me quality, safety, allergens, menus, production plans, chef and KA/KP planning, three meal handovers, stock, ordering, waste, food cost, In-Charge development, Reliever development, team training, and the staffing requirement to Shannon and Louise.

That is the correct Head Chef scope — but only if Chef 2 and the Reliever have real authority, and only if management time is on the rota. As written, the script still makes one person the system. If I am off, the paper does not say who may change a menu, hold a dish, authorise an order or refuse an unsafe cross-deployment.

**Fix:** name the deputy authority on every shift. When I am not on duty, the In-Charge or Reliever leading the shift owns the pass, the handover and the day’s Kitchen decisions within the 7-day plan.

### 4.3 Three different chef names are used, and none are tied to the shift table

The script uses **Head Chef**, **Kitchen In-Charge / Chef 2**, **Skilled Reliever Chef**, then **Morning Chef** and **Late / Dinner Chef**.

It never says which person works 07:00–16:00, which person works 12:00–21:00, or whether I am supernumerary to those two shifts. A rota clerk cannot build a week from this. An employee cannot see their contracted pattern.

**Fix:** one naming set. Head Chef, In-Charge and Reliever are **people / posts**. Morning and Late are **shifts**. The weekly rota names the post on each shift.

### 4.4 The Reliever Chef is required for the pilot measures, but is not a real post yet

Section 2 says the Reliever must be able to cover either main chef shift to the same standard. Section 10 says normal weekly off-days and short absence must be covered without excessive hours. Section 11 then asks, after four weeks, whether the Reliever should be external or developed internally.

Those three statements cannot stand together. If the Reliever is only a hope for week 5, the week 1–4 resilience measure will fail by design. I will cover the off-days again, which is the problem the paper says it is solving.

**Fix:** before day one, name the Reliever, confirm they can run both shifts, and put them on the 4-week rota. If no internal person is ready, say so now and agree interim cover. Do not run a resilience pilot without a reliever.

### 4.5 KA/KP headcount is three different ideas at once

The script says:

- the original model allowed **three full-time KA/KP posts**;
- the pilot should confirm **actual hours** before treating that as permanent;
- the key requirement is **coverage of windows and relief**, not everyone working together every day;
- there is an Early window, a Late window, and a Relief/Float.

That is three models in one paragraph: a 3-FTE establishment, a “wait and see” establishment, and a float principle. Relief/Float is described both as a **window** and as a **post**. KA and KP are never defined. Kitchen Assistant and Kitchen Porter are different skills. Mixing them lets us roster a porter as if they were a prep assistant, or the reverse.

At **20 guests**, opening as three permanent full-time support posts is likely to be heavy unless the three posts exist to cover a 7-day week (not to put three people on the same Tuesday). The script never shows a 7-day worked example, so nobody can see which it is.

**Fix:** define KA and KP separately. Roster the two support **windows** first. Use Relief only for days off, sickness, leave and a named peak. Confirm after four weeks of **20-guest** hours whether the permanent mix is two posts plus casual relief, or three posts for 7-day cover.

### 4.6 The four-hour chef overlap is justified for 30–35 covers, not for 20

07:00–16:00 and 12:00–21:00 give 18 chef-hours a day and a four-hour overlap. The draft says the overlap is for handover, prep, quality control and management time. That can be right — **if** the overlap is used that way.

At 20 guests, two chefs both producing lunch for four hours will idle skilled time and contradict “better use of skilled hours”. The paper gives no rule for what the second chef does between 12:00 and 16:00 on a 20-guest day.

**Fix:** keep the clock times if they fit breakfast and dinner. Write the overlap rule: one chef owns lunch production; the other uses the block for handover, quality, tomorrow’s prep and the Head Chef planning work. Do not both stand on a 20-cover lunch unless a retreat peak or a training service is named that day.

### 4.7 Head Chef planning time is hoped for, not rostered

Section 7 says “approximately 2–3 hours of management/admin work can be completed within the normal operational shift” where overlap and KA/KP “make it practical”. A weekly admin day “may be used where agreed”.

“Where practical” and “may be used” are how planning time disappears. On a 20-guest day the time should exist. If it is not a named block on the rota, it will still be eaten by wash-up, a late delivery or a Restaurant request.

The planning stack is also heavy for a 20-guest house if there is no template: 4-week outlook, 7-day plan, 48-hour confirmation, daily shift plan. I support the 7-day and 48-hour plans. I do not support four documents with no owner, no form and no place they live.

**Fix:** roster a protected planning block in the overlap (or a named weekly admin morning) with outputs: 7-day plan, 48-hour confirmation, order list, support requirement. Give us the template in week one. The 4-week outlook is visibility only — retreat diary and approximate numbers — not a fourth full plan.

### 4.8 Ownership leaks will put Restaurant work back onto Kitchen

Section 6 is mostly right. The leaks are where employment arguments will start.

- **Fruit.** Restaurant owns refreshment fruit; Kitchen owns fruit in meals and desserts. In a real store that is the same fruit, the same knife and the same fridge. Without a labelled par and a named cutter, Kitchen will keep cutting it.
- **Restaurant wash-up.** “Kitchen support may help temporarily when directed and capacity allows” is how temporary help becomes the daily method. At 20 guests we will often *look* free after lunch. That must not become routine FOH wash.
- **“When Kitchen work is complete.”** Who says it is complete? The chef leading the shift must say so, against a close checklist. If Duty Manager and Head Chef disagree, we need a same-day rule, not only a GM appeal after the damage is done.
- **Shared equipment.** If the dishwasher or scullery is shared, the script must say who owns it at 15:00 and at 21:00. Silence here is how Kitchen inherits the dining room.
- **Tea/coffee milk versus Kitchen dairy.** Separate par, separate order, or it returns to my list.

**Fix:** physical or labelled split for refreshment stock; a close checklist that defines “Kitchen work complete”; Restaurant wash-up is Restaurant work unless the Duty Manager records a one-off move.

### 4.9 Duty Manager versus Head Chef is not tight enough for live service

Shannon and Louise coordinate deployment and “do not override genuine food-safety/technical requirements”. The script never defines **genuine**. Without a written minima list, every staffing disagreement can be framed as food safety, or food safety can be overridden as “just being helpful”.

There is also no live RACI: who calls the pass, who delays a service, who 86’s a dish, who tells Restaurant the buffet is late. Section 6 says there is “no separate Duty Manager role” and then says Shannon and Louise **are** the Duty Managers. That sentence will confuse anyone reading the paper as an employment document. It means “we will not hire a third Duty Manager post”. It should say that.

Escalation to the GM is right for a deadlock. A 4-week pilot also needs a **same-day** holding rule: the food-safety position stands until the GM decides.

**Fix:** attach a one-page Kitchen minima list (allergens, temperatures, two-chef close-to-open rule, no Kitchen left without a competent chef). Write the live-service RACI. Clarify that Shannon and Louise are the Duty Managers; no extra DM post is proposed.

### 4.10 Ordering and stock is a principle, not a system

“Agreed list/system”, “authorised staff”, “2–3 authorised people” — none are named. Stock that depends on unnamed people still depends on one person. Restaurant and Kitchen stock are split on paper only.

There are no par levels, no order cut-off times, no supplier list, no food-cost target, and no waste record. Those are employment and control tools, not extras.

**Fix:** name the list, the authorised orderers (at least two plus me), the pars for the 20-guest menu, and the weekly waste/food-cost check. Train the second and third person before the pilot, not after someone is off.

### 4.11 Pilot measures are not measurable

| Measure in the script | Problem |
|-----------------------|---------|
| Ready for planned service time | Service times are not in the paper. No grace, no late-minute log. |
| Agreed food quality | Agreed with whom, scored how? |
| No labour reduction compromises safety | A constraint, not a measure. |
| Off-days covered without excessive hours | “Excessive” is not defined. No overtime cap, no TOIL rule. |
| Chefs not “routinely” used for washing | “Routinely” is undefined. |
| Extra hour has a recorded reason | Where, and who writes it? |
| 7-day and 48-hour plans are “clear enough” | No template, so “clear enough” cannot be audited. |

Missing, and needed for a fair 20-guest pilot: allergen/incident log, waste, food-cost movement, overtime hours, on-time service count, and a mid-pilot review at week 2. Four weeks is long enough to embed a wrong rota.

**Fix:** attach a one-page scorecard. Example: meals ready on time Y/N; any allergen or safety incident; overtime hours; chefs used for Restaurant wash Y/N; 7-day plan issued by the agreed day; extra-hour reason logged. Review at week 2 and week 4.

### 4.12 The decision section has no pass mark

Section 11 says adopt, adjust hours, confirm the Reliever, confirm the KA/KP mix, and do not add permanent headcount without a recurring need. The simple rule at the end is right.

There is no threshold. What is “consistently achieved”? 20 on-time services out of 21? Zero safety incidents? A maximum number of extra hours? Without numbers, the week-4 meeting will be opinion.

**Fix:** agree the pass marks before day one, using the 20-guest scorecard.

### 4.13 The script is missing the work that employment documents must contain

A Kitchen operating proposal used for employment, rota and a 4-week trial should state, and currently does not:

- breakfast, lunch and dinner **service times**;
- menu style (retreat buffet, plated, or mixed) and diet rules (vegetarian line, allergen process, how guest diets reach the Kitchen);
- opening and closing checklists;
- a worked **7-day rota** at 20 guests showing off-days and the close-to-open rule;
- break entitlement on 8- and 9-hour shifts;
- Working Time rest: 21:00 finish to 07:00 start is **10 hours**, not the 11-hour daily rest. The script is right to forbid that rostering; it should say it is a legal rest issue, not only a kindness;
- pay, overtime or TOIL for recorded extra hours;
- induction and competency sign-off for In-Charge, Reliever and KA/KP;
- order cut-off and delivery days;
- what happens if a week jumps from 20 toward 30 guests.

Until those are attached, this is a direction paper, not an employment model.

### 4.14 Language and structure problems that will cause arguments later

- **KA/KP** is used as one job. It is two.
- **“Original pilot model”** is referred to and not attached. Readers cannot see what is being tested against.
- **Morning / Late shifts** do not state who holds Head Chef authority on each.
- **Late Chef 12:00–21:00** may be tight if dinner service runs past 20:00. The paper never gives dinner time, so 21:00 close is an assumption.
- **Morning Chef 07:00** may be tight if breakfast is at 07:30 and includes a cooked offer. The paper never gives breakfast time.
- **Shannon / Louise** are named as if the reader already knows the employment structure. For a document that will be used with the Kitchen team, their Duty Manager scope should be one short paragraph.
- Section 6’s “no separate Duty Manager role” reads as if Duty Manager work has been removed. It has not.

None of these are hostile points. They are why a well-meant script fails on the first rota.
