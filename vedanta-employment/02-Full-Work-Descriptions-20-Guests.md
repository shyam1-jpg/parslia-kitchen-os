# The Vedanta

## Kitchen Full Work Descriptions — 20-Guest Baseline

**Department:** Kitchen, Hospitality  
**Working baseline:** 20 guests (not 30–35)  
**Use:** Employment, rota, induction and the 4-week pilot  
**Classification:** Employment and kitchen operations only. This is not a software, app or product document.

These descriptions sit under the Kitchen Employment Response. They replace the short bullet lists in the draft proposal.

**Common rules for every Kitchen post**

- The working house is **20 guests**. A higher retreat week is a recorded peak, not the normal job size.
- Kitchen owns food production, food safety, Kitchen stock, Kitchen pot wash and Kitchen cleaning.
- Restaurant owns guest-facing dining, buffet presentation, tea and coffee, refreshment fruit and routine FOH wash-up.
- No cross-deployment may put allergens, hygiene or technical standards at risk.
- A chef who finishes at 21:00 is not rostered to start at 07:00 the next day.
- Extra hours and temporary support moves are recorded with a reason on the day.

## 1. Head Chef — Shyam

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Job title | Head Chef |
| Post holder | Shyam |
| Department | Kitchen |
| Reports to | General Manager |
| Works with | Kitchen In-Charge, Reliever Chef, KA/KP team, Duty Managers (Shannon and Louise), Restaurant lead |
| Responsible for | Technical Kitchen standard, food quality, food safety, menus, production planning, Kitchen stock and orders, training, Kitchen staffing requirement |
| Contract pattern (pilot) | Rostered chef shifts plus a protected planning block inside the ordinary week. Not an add-on after a full operational day. |
| Baseline | 20 guests |

### 1.1 Purpose of the role

Lead the Kitchen as a technical section of Hospitality. Plan the food and the Kitchen team for 20 guests so breakfast, lunch and dinner are ready, safe and consistent. Build the In-Charge and Reliever so the Kitchen does not depend on one person. Do not absorb Restaurant, reception, housekeeping or Duty Manager work.

### 1.2 Daily duties

1. Read the day picture: 20-guest count, arrivals, allergens, diet notes, service times and any retreat change.
2. Confirm the menu, production quantities and named dish owners for the chef on duty.
3. Brief the Kitchen team at the start of the relevant shift (menu, allergens, timings, support tasks).
4. Own or oversee production of the meals on the shifts you are rostered to lead.
5. Check food quality, presentation, temperatures, labelling and allergen control before handover to Restaurant.
6. Hand meals to Restaurant at the agreed time. Kitchen work stops at a ready, labelled, safe handover — not at guest-facing buffet dressing.
7. Review the shortage / order list. Authorise or place Kitchen orders due that day.
8. See that Kitchen pot wash, surfaces and the production close are done to standard on your shift.
9. Take and give a written or board handover: leftover use, allergens, stock, equipment faults, next-meal prep.
10. Report equipment or building faults to Estates. Do not own the repair.
11. Record any extra hour or support movement and the reason.
12. Use the protected planning block, when rostered, for the 7-day plan, 48-hour confirmation, orders, training notes or the support requirement for Duty Managers.

### 1.3 Weekly and periodic duties

1. Issue a **7-day plan**: menu, 20-guest quantities, prep owners, stock and orders, chef and KA/KP allocation, allergens, risks, support requirement.
2. Issue a **48-hour confirmation**: final count, substitutions, deliveries, service timing, any targeted support change.
3. Keep a **4-week outlook** as visibility only: known retreats, approximate numbers, diet risk, supplier risk.
4. Train and assess the In-Charge and Reliever against the same technical standard.
5. Review waste, food-cost movement and recurring bottlenecks with the GM as required.
6. Give Shannon and Louise the Kitchen staffing and support requirement in time for the rota. They deploy. They do not override a genuine food-safety or technical requirement.
7. Review competency sign-off for new or relief KA/KP staff before they work unsupervised.

### 1.4 What this role does not do

- Restaurant buffet setup, table clearing, dining-room reset or guest refreshment service.
- Stocking tea, coffee, FOH milk, sugar, biscuits or refreshment fruit.
- Routine Restaurant crockery and mug washing.
- Reception, guest administration, housekeeping or Estates maintenance.
- Acting as Duty Manager for the wider building.

### 1.5 Success in the role (20-guest pilot)

- Breakfast, lunch and dinner handed over on time.
- Agreed quality and presentation held.
- No safety or allergen failure caused by labour or shortcut.
- Weekly off-days held; extra hours recorded and not routine.
- 7-day and 48-hour plans issued in time for staffing and ordering.
- In-Charge and Reliever can lead a shift without the Head Chef standing behind every dish.

## 2. Kitchen In-Charge / Chef 2

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Job title | Kitchen In-Charge (Chef 2) |
| Department | Kitchen |
| Reports to | Head Chef |
| Responsible for | Day-to-day production lead on the shifts they are rostered to run |
| Baseline | 20 guests |

### 2.1 Purpose of the role

Run the operational floor when rostered as the leading chef. Deliver the Head Chef’s menu and plan to the same standard. Own the shift: prep, service readiness, quality checks and handover. This is a deputy post with real authority on the shift, not a helper who waits for Shyam on every decision.

### 2.2 Daily duties

1. Take the handover and the day’s 20-guest plan, allergens and dish list.
2. Run mise en place for the meals on that shift.
3. Cook and finish dishes to the agreed recipes and presentation.
4. Check temperatures, labels, allergen separation and hygiene during production.
5. Have food ready for the agreed handover time and pass it to Restaurant.
6. Direct the KA/KP on the shift: prep support, pot wash, labelling, cleaning — not Restaurant work unless the Head Chef or Duty Manager has recorded a one-off move and Kitchen work is complete.
7. Complete Kitchen close tasks for that shift: wash-up of production equipment, surfaces, labels, waste, next-meal prep.
8. Write the handover: what is left, what is short, what the next chef must know.
9. Escalate genuine food-safety or staffing risk to the Head Chef, or to the Duty Manager if the Head Chef is off, without standing down the safety position.
10. When the Head Chef is off, authorise routine Kitchen decisions inside the 7-day plan, including holding a dish and recording a shortage.

### 2.3 What this role does not do

- Redesign the menu against the Head Chef’s plan, except for a safety or shortage substitution that is recorded.
- Own Restaurant service or refreshment.
- Override the close-to-open rest rule to “help”.

### 2.4 Success in the role

The shift runs to time and standard when the Head Chef is not on the floor. Handover is complete. KA/KP are used for Kitchen support, not as a default FOH crew.

## 3. Skilled Reliever Chef

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Job title | Skilled Reliever Chef |
| Department | Kitchen |
| Reports to | Head Chef |
| Responsible for | Cover of either main chef shift to the same technical standard |
| Baseline | 20 guests |

### 3.1 Purpose of the role

Give the Kitchen resilience. Cover Head Chef or In-Charge off-days, short absence and leave. The Reliever must be able to run **either** 07:00–16:00 **or** 12:00–21:00 without a drop in safety, quality or timing.

This post must be **named and competent before the 4-week pilot**, or the resilience measure has nothing to test.

### 3.2 Daily duties (when covering)

1. Carry out the full leading-chef work description for the shift being covered (see In-Charge daily duties).
2. Follow the issued 7-day plan and 48-hour confirmation.
3. Keep recipes, allergen control and handover at the same standard as a normal day.
4. Do not leave unfinished production, unlabelled food or an incomplete close because “I am only covering”.
5. Record any substitution, shortage or extra hour.

### 3.3 Development duties (when not covering)

1. Work designated services with the Head Chef or In-Charge to sign off both shift patterns.
2. Learn the order list, par levels and supplier routine so absence of one person does not stop Kitchen ordering.
3. Complete competency sign-off for breakfast, lunch, dinner, close and allergen control.

### 3.4 What this role does not do

- Exist only on paper as a week-5 decision while the Head Chef covers every off-day in weeks 1–4.
- Be used as extra KA/KP labour on a 20-guest day when a chef is already on the shift.

### 3.5 Success in the role

Off-days and short absence are covered without excessive hours on the remaining chef. Quality and safety do not dip on Reliever-led shifts.

## 4. Kitchen Assistant / Kitchen Porter — Early support

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Job title | Kitchen Assistant / Kitchen Porter — Early |
| Typical window | 07:00–15:00 |
| Department | Kitchen |
| Reports to | Chef leading the morning shift (Head Chef, In-Charge or Reliever) |
| Responsible for | Morning Kitchen support for a 20-guest house |
| Baseline | 20 guests |

### 4.1 Purpose of the role

Support breakfast and lunch production so the chef’s skilled time is used on food, not on porter work. Cover wash-up, deliveries, stock movement, labelling and Kitchen cleaning in the morning window.

**KA work** is prep assistance under the chef: peel, cut, weigh, label, portion, stock away, as directed.  
**KP work** is pot wash, dish wash of Kitchen equipment, bins, floors and scheduled cleaning.  
The rota must say which skill is required that day. They are not the same job.

### 4.2 Daily duties

1. Clock in, uniform, hand wash, read the day board (covers, allergens, chef instructions).
2. Assist morning prep for breakfast and lunch as directed — 20-guest quantities, not a 35-cover batch.
3. Receive deliveries with the chef or authorised person: check, date, put away, FIFO.
4. Move stock, label containers, keep the kitchen walkways clear.
5. Wash pots, pans and Kitchen production equipment through the morning and after lunch.
6. Clean Kitchen surfaces, sinks and allocated areas. Follow clean-as-you-go.
7. Do not start Restaurant buffet dressing, tea-station fruit or FOH mug washing as a routine task.
8. If Kitchen work is complete and the leading chef confirms it, you may be moved to an agreed non-technical bottleneck. That move is recorded.

### 4.3 Success in the role

Chef is not pulled onto repetitive wash-up or delivery runs during breakfast and lunch. Kitchen is clean, labelled and ready for the late window handover.

## 5. Kitchen Assistant / Kitchen Porter — Late support

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Job title | Kitchen Assistant / Kitchen Porter — Late |
| Typical window | 15:00–22:00 |
| Department | Kitchen |
| Reports to | Chef leading the late / dinner shift |
| Responsible for | Dinner support, Kitchen pot wash and Kitchen close |
| Baseline | 20 guests |

### 5.1 Purpose of the role

Support dinner production and close the Kitchen to a safe standard so the late chef is not left with porter work after service.

### 5.2 Daily duties

1. Take handover from the early support and the morning chef: leftover use, soak tanks, deliveries still out, cleaning left.
2. Assist dinner prep as directed at 20-guest quantities.
3. Run Kitchen pot wash through dinner and close.
4. Complete the Kitchen close list: equipment, surfaces, floors, bins, labels, fridges wiped as required, lights/equipment left as the close-down says.
5. Do not take on dining-room clear-down or Restaurant crockery as a routine finish. If Kitchen close is done and the leading chef confirms capacity, a recorded one-off may be agreed.
6. Leave a clean, safe kitchen for the 07:00 chef. Do not create a close-to-open problem by leaving production work for 07:00 that should have been finished at 21:00.

### 5.3 Success in the role

Dinner support is real, not cosmetic. Kitchen close is finished inside the planned window. The morning chef does not inherit last night’s pots.

## 6. Kitchen Assistant / Kitchen Porter — Relief / Float

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Job title | Kitchen Assistant / Kitchen Porter — Relief / Float |
| Typical use | Days off, sickness, annual leave, named peak bottlenecks |
| Department | Kitchen |
| Reports to | Head Chef for allocation; chef leading the shift when on duty |
| Baseline | 20 guests |

### 6.1 Purpose of the role

Cover the early or late **window** when the regular support is off, and attend a named peak (for example a 20-to-higher guest jump, a deep-clean day, or a heavy delivery). This is not a third person standing in the kitchen every day “in case”.

Hours flex with actual 20-guest demand. The 4-week pilot must show whether this is a permanent post, a casual bank, or hours already absorbed by the two windows.

### 6.2 Duties when called

1. Cover the early or late work description in full.
2. Or complete a named task list: deep clean, delivery day, wash-up spike, stock day.
3. Do not invent extra work on a quiet 20-guest day to fill the shift.

### 6.3 Success in the role

Support windows remain covered when someone is off. Extra hours on the regular KA/KP and chefs do not become the default cover method.

## 7. How the posts sit on a 20-guest day

These are **shifts**, not extra job titles.

| Shift | Clock | Who may hold it | What the second skilled person does in overlap |
|-------|-------|-----------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| Morning chef | 07:00–16:00 | Head Chef, In-Charge or Reliever | — |
| Late / dinner chef | 12:00–21:00 | Head Chef, In-Charge or Reliever | — |
| Overlap 12:00–16:00 | 4 hours | Both chefs on duty that day | One chef owns lunch. The other does handover, quality, next-meal prep and Head Chef planning. They do not both produce a 20-cover lunch unless a peak or training service is named. |
| Early support | 07:00–15:00 | KA and/or KP as rostered | Kitchen support only unless a recorded move is agreed. |
| Late support | 15:00–22:00 | KA and/or KP as rostered | Dinner support and Kitchen close. |
| Relief | As required | Relief / Float | Cover a window or a named peak only. |

A chef finishing at 21:00 is not rostered to the 07:00 shift the next morning.

## 8. Duty Manager interface (not a Kitchen post)

Shannon and Louise are the Duty Managers for the wider Hospitality operation. This is not a Kitchen job and no extra Duty Manager post is proposed.

| They do | They do not |
|---------|-------------|
| Review the whole house and coordinate live deployment | Override a genuine Kitchen food-safety or technical requirement |
| Receive the Head Chef’s weekly support requirement | Redesign the menu, the 20-guest production plan or the Kitchen close standard |
| Record a one-off move of KA/KP to a non-technical bottleneck when Kitchen work is complete | Treat Kitchen as the default Restaurant wash-up crew |
| Escalate a deadlock to the GM | Leave a shift without a competent chef in order to cover FOH |

Until the GM decides a deadlock, the food-safety position set by the chef leading the shift stands.

## 9. Employment notes for the 4-week pilot

1. Issue these work descriptions to each post holder and keep a signed copy.
2. Do not recruit or confirm three permanent KA/KP posts on a 30–35 assumption. Confirm from **20-guest** hours.
3. Name the Reliever and at least two authorised Kitchen orderers before day one.
4. Attach service times, the close checklist and the week-1 rota to this pack when the GM agrees them.
5. Review at week 2 and week 4 against the 20-guest scorecard in the professional response.

**Kitchen owns the food and the technical Kitchen standard. Restaurant owns the guest-facing dining and refreshment service.**
