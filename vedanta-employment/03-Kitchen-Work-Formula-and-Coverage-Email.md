## 1. What one “dish” really is

One dish on the menu is not one job.

A single dish can have **around ten elements** before it reaches Restaurant:

1. Collect and check the ingredient (date, quality, allergen).  
2. Wash.  
3. Peel / trim.  
4. Cut / chop to the recipe size.  
5. Weigh / measure to the recipe.  
6. Pre-cook or blanch.  
7. Make the sauce, dressing or finish.  
8. Garnish or second component.  
9. Plate or tray for handover.  
10. Wash the pot, board and tray — then label and date what is left.

From **starter to dessert** we are not doing one item. We are doing a set: soup, salad, main, side, sauce, dessert — often **six different items**, sometimes more. Each one has its own recipe. Each recipe has to be **calculated for that day’s guests**, then every ingredient **collected**, then **prepped**, then cooked.

If six items each need about ten elements:

**6 items × 10 elements = 60 kitchen jobs**  
before we even multiply by guest number, extra diets, washing, date checks, menu typing and close.

That is why “20 guests, so it should be light” is not how a kitchen counts work.

## 2. The formula (how I programme the day)

I do not guess the day. I programme it in this order.

### Step 1 — Open safe

- Clock in, uniform, hands.  
- Fridge / freezer temperatures.  
- **Date check** every open label (use-by, cooked-on, defrost).  
- Spot-check stock that will be used today. Pull anything out of date.  
- Note equipment faults and report them.

This is HACCP. It is every day, 10 guests or 50.

### Step 2 — Lock the real number and the real diets

- Confirm **today’s guest count**. It can be 10, 20, 30, 50 — and it can change.  
- Confirm **allergens and extra diets**. These often arrive as add-ons.  
- Write them down (board or printed list). Verbal only at the pass is how mistakes happen.  
- If a diet is added after prep has started, that is a **new line of work**, not a garnish.

### Step 3 — Menu for *this* house, *this* day

- Set or confirm the menu **to the guest and to the staff who are actually in**.  
- If I have no support, the menu must be simpler. If I have 50 guests and six full items, I need hands.  
- **Type the menu. Print the menu.** That is Kitchen time: checking spelling, diet tags, service order.

### Step 4 — Calculate every recipe

For each dish:

- Take the recipe.  
- Recalculate **every ingredient** for today’s number (not last week’s 50, not a guess).  
- Add the extra diet versions (gluten-free, dairy-free, no onion/garlic, allergen swap).  
- Write a pick-list.

**Formula:**

`Ingredient qty today = recipe qty per portion × confirmed guests × yield factor`  
then **plus** each extra diet line.

If we skip this step we either waste food or run out in service.

### Step 5 — Collect, then prep (do not cook from an empty board)

- Collect every ingredient on the pick-list.  
- Only then start the ten elements: wash, cut, weigh, label.  
- Prep is the long part. Cooking is the visible part. Support staff exist for this, not for decoration.

### Step 6 — Produce the three services

Same cycle for **breakfast, lunch and dinner**:

- Finish to the recipe.  
- Temperature and allergen check.  
- Handover to Restaurant on time.  
- Kitchen pot wash and reset for the next service.

Restaurant owns guest-facing buffet and refreshment. I own the food being ready and safe.

### Step 7 — Order, plan, print, clean

Every day, not “when it is quiet”:

- Shortages onto the order list.  
- Planning the next 48 hours against the next retreat number (which may jump).  
- Menu typing / printing for the next service if it has changed.  
- Cleaning, labelling, date check again at close.  
- Deep-clean tasks when the day’s food work is actually finished.

### Step 8 — Close, or keep going if the board is empty

If there is a second chef or KA/KP, close sits inside the planned shift.

If there is **no one on the Kitchen board**, the same person who opened at 07:00 is still date-checking, washing and finishing dinner. That is how a day becomes **07:00–19:00**, or **07:00–21:00**. That is not a staffing model. That is a gap I have been covering.

## 3. How guest number changes the load

Batching helps a little. It does not make 50 the same as 20.

| Confirmed guests | Guest factor I use | What it means on the floor |
|------------------|--------------------|----------------------------|
| 10 | 1.0 | Full HACCP, full menu cycle, fewer portions. Diet add-ons still hurt. |
| 20 | 1.6 | Normal Vedanta working house. Two services overlap. Wash and prep are constant. |
| 30 | 2.2 | More trays, more diet lines, longer pot wash. One chef cannot also be the porter. |
| 50 | 3.2 | Retreat peak. Every element is multiplied. Close runs late unless support is real. |

**Diet add-on:** each extra diet or allergen version is treated as **+0.5 to +1.0 extra dish**, because it is a separate collect, prep, cook and label — not a spoon from the main pot.

**Work score for one service (simple):**

`Work score = (menu items × elements per item × guest factor) + diet add-ons`

Example, lunch at 20 guests, 6 items, 10 elements, 3 extra diet lines:

`6 × 10 × 1.6 = 96`  
`+ 3 diet lines ≈ 99`

That is why I need support on a “only 20 guests” day. The guests are 20. The jobs are not 20.

## 4. Coverage options — recommended and leaner if budget is tight

I am not asking for the same brigade every day. I am asking for **the right cover for the number and the menu**. If you think a line is over-given, use the **lean option** in that row — not “no support and Shyam covers 07:00–21:00”.

### 10 guests

**Recommended:** 1 chef for the day + a **short KA/KP window** (4 hours) over the dirty period (after breakfast into lunch wash, or dinner close).

**If budget is tight:** 1 chef alone, **only if** the menu is cut (fewer items / fewer elements) and diets are known the day before. No surprise 50-cover prep.

**Different idea:** chef 08:00–17:00; no late chef. Dinner is a simpler, mostly prepped menu. Restaurant does all FOH wash. I will not also cut the morning date-check.

**Do not cut:** HACCP open, recipe calculate, allergen list.

### 20 guests (normal house)

**Recommended:** Morning chef 07:00–16:00 + late chef 12:00–21:00. One chef owns lunch; the other uses the overlap for handover, next prep and ordering. Early KA/KP 07:00–15:00 **or** late KA/KP 15:00–22:00 — at least one full support window.

**If budget is tight:** 1 chef + **1 KA/KP split across the dirty hours** (e.g. 10:00–14:00 and 17:00–21:00 as two short blocks, or one 08:00–16:00 KA). Second chef only on the heavier day of the week, not every day.

**Different idea:** keep two chef shifts on retreat-change days only; midweek 20-guest with a stable menu = 1 chef + 1 KA. Menu stays at 20-guest quantities, not a 50-guest batch “in case”.

**Do not cut:** a second pair of hands for wash/prep on at least one window. Do not roster the same chef 21:00 then 07:00.

### 30 guests

**Recommended:** Two chef shifts as above + **both** KA/KP windows (early and late). Diets and pot wash at 30 covers will bury one chef.

**If budget is tight:** two chefs + **one** KA/KP on the heavier window (usually lunch wash into dinner prep). No third support body. Menu: drop one extra element per dish (fewer garnishes / fewer composed desserts).

**Different idea:** 30-guest week = two chefs; KA/KP as a **casual / bank shift** on arrival day and departure day only.

**Do not cut:** two competent chefs when diets are stacking. One chef at 30 covers with six full items is how 21:00 close happens.

### 50 guests (peak / big retreat)

**Recommended:** Two chefs + early KA/KP + late KA/KP + extra hours on delivery day. Menu planned two days ahead. Diet list locked 48 hours out if the office can give it.

**If budget is tight:** two chefs + two KA/KP windows, **no extra FTE**. Use paid extra hours only on the two peak days, recorded. Simplify the menu: still safe and good, fewer components.

**Different idea:** bring a **casual KP** for wash only on the 50-guest days (cheaper than a third chef; protects skilled time). Or drop to a tighter menu (soup + main + dessert, salad as a simple line) for that week only.

**Do not cut:** both support windows on a 50-guest day. Do not ask one person to open at 07:00 and finish a 50-cover dinner.

## 5. When there is nobody on the Kitchen board

This is the problem I need named, not left as “Shyam will do it”.

| Situation | What happens now | What I need instead |
|-----------|------------------|---------------------|
| No KA/KP on the board | I cook and I wash. Close moves to 19:00–21:00. | A short wash/prep window, or a simpler menu that day. |
| No second chef | I cover 07:00 through dinner. | A reliever or In-Charge on the opposite shift, or dinner simplified and an earlier close. |
| Number jumps 20 → 50 | Same staff, three times the trays. | 48-hour warning + casual KP or extra hours, recorded. |
| Diets added on the day | New full prep line in the middle of service. | Office / Restaurant send diets in writing the day before. Late add-ons mean a simpler substitute, not a new à-la-carte line with no time. |

**Rule I will hold:** if there is no support, I will still run a **safe** menu. I will not run a 50-guest, six-item, ten-element menu as if the brigade were full. That is food safety and quality, not attitude.
