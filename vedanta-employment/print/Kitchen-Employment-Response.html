This is a professional response to The Vedanta — Kitchen Operating Proposal — Shyam, 4-Week Pilot. I support the direction. I also need the guest figure, the work descriptions and several gaps in the script corrected before we treat the paper as a working employment model.

1. Positive response

Thank you for putting this on paper. The draft is the first document that treats the Kitchen as a technical section with a clear boundary, rather than a place that absorbs every hospitality gap. That is the right starting point, and I welcome it.

  • Technical ownership is named. Food quality, food safety, allergens, menus, production planning, stock and meal readiness sit with the Kitchen. Shannon and Louise can coordinate people without taking food-safety decisions they should not own.
  • The Restaurant boundary is the right one. Buffet presentation, table clearing, dining-room reset, tea and coffee, guest refreshment fruit and routine restaurant crockery washing are not Kitchen work.
  • Chef relief and succession are named as a requirement. Developing an In-Charge and a Reliever, so the operation does not depend on one or two people, is the professional way forward.
  • Protected management time is the right instinct. Head Chef work is not whatever is left after service.
  • One Hospitality team, with a food-safety line that cannot be crossed, is sound. If we cannot agree a deployment, the GM decides.
  • Prove the hours before we add headcount is fair. I do not want ghost posts. I do want enough cover to run a safe 20-guest kitchen without excessive hours.
  • The close-to-open rule is correct. A chef finishing at 21:00 must not be rostered onto the 07:00 shift the next morning.

I want this proposal to succeed. For it to succeed as an employment document, the guest number must be the real number, each role must have a full work description, and the gaps in the script must be fixed before week one.

2. Required correction: 20 guests, not 30–35

Every staffing, overlap, support and “skilled hours” assumption in the draft is built on a 30–35 guest baseline.

The correct working baseline for this kitchen, and for this pilot, is 20 guests.

That is not a small edit. It changes the model.

Two chef shifts sized for 30–35 Clock times may still work; do not staff as a 35-cover brigade A 4-hour daily overlap on 20 lunches wastes skilled hours unless it is used for handover and Head Chef planning.
Three full-time KA/KP posts as the original model Confirm actual hours at 20 guests Support windows still matter. Permanent headcount must follow 20-guest demand.

Please replace 30–35 with 20 guests wherever the draft uses it. If a retreat week later rises toward 30 guests, that is a peak variant, not the baseline.

3. Full work description — Head Chef

The draft lists bullets under “Shyam’s Role”. That is a purpose note, not an employment work description. Full descriptions for every Kitchen post are in the companion file 02-Full-Work-Descriptions-20-Guests. The Head Chef description is set out here so this response stands alone.

Purpose. Own the Kitchen technically at a 20-guest baseline: food quality, food safety, menus, production, stock and chef standards. Lead and plan the Kitchen. Do not routinely carry Restaurant work, Duty Manager work, or every operational gap in the building.

Reports to. General Manager for employment, performance and final dispute. Works with Shannon and Louise on live deployment. They do not override a genuine food-safety or technical Kitchen requirement.

Daily work at 20 guests

  • Confirm guest count, allergens, diet notes and service times for the day.
  • Set or confirm the day’s menu, quantities and named dish owners.
  • See that breakfast, lunch and dinner are produced to standard and handed to Restaurant on time.
  • Check temperatures, labelling, allergen control and production hygiene.
  • Oversee Kitchen pot wash, Kitchen cleaning and the technical close for the shift pattern in force.
  • Review shortages and the order list; authorise or place Kitchen orders as required that day.
  • Brief the chef on duty and the KA/KP support; take a proper handover.
  • Record any unplanned extra hour or support move, with the reason.
  • Complete the Head Chef planning block when the rota has protected it — menu, 7-day plan, orders, training notes — inside the ordinary shift.

Weekly work

  • Publish a 7-day plan: menu, 20-guest quantities, prep owners, stock and orders, chef and KA/KP allocation, allergens, risks and the support requirement.
  • Confirm the 48-hour picture: final count, substitutions, deliveries and any targeted support change.
  • Hold a short training or standards session with the In-Charge and Reliever.
  • Review waste, food-cost movement and recurring bottlenecks.
  • Give Shannon and Louise a clear staffing and support requirement. They coordinate deployment. They do not redesign the Kitchen.

What this role does not own

  • Restaurant buffet setup, guest-facing presentation, table clearing or dining-room reset.
  • Tea, coffee, refreshment area, guest biscuits, refreshment fruit or FOH milk and alternatives.
  • Routine Restaurant crockery and mug washing.
  • Reception, check-in, housekeeping or Estates work. Kitchen reports faults; Estates owns the fix.
  • The Duty Manager function. Shannon and Louise hold that.

4. Issues found in the script

The script is well intended. It is not yet safe to issue as an employment or rota document.

4.1 The guest number is wrong, so the whole model is sized for the wrong house

The purpose, the chef-shift heading and the measures table all say 30–35 guests. This kitchen’s working baseline is 20 guests. At 35 covers, a four-hour chef overlap and three KA/KP posts can be argued. At 20 covers, the same structure will look overstaffed on a quiet day and will not tell us the truth about actual hours required. If we pilot the 35-cover model on a 20-guest house, we will either waste skilled hours or conclude we need people we do not need.

4.2 “Shyam should not carry every gap” is then contradicted by the same section

Section 1 says I should lead and plan rather than routinely carry every operational gap. The same section then gives me quality, safety, allergens, menus, production, staffing plans, three meal handovers, stock, waste, food cost, In-Charge development, Reliever development, team training, and the staffing requirement to the Duty Managers. That is the correct Head Chef scope — but only if Chef 2 and the Reliever have real authority, and only if management time is on the rota. As written, if I am off, the paper does not say who may change a menu, hold a dish, authorise an order or refuse an unsafe cross-deployment.

4.3 Three different chef names are used, and none are tied to the shift table

The script uses Head Chef, Kitchen In-Charge / Chef 2, Skilled Reliever Chef, then Morning Chef and Late / Dinner Chef. It never says which person works 07:00–16:00 or 12:00–21:00, or whether I am supernumerary to those two shifts. A rota clerk cannot build a week from this. An employee cannot see their contracted pattern. Posts are people. Morning and Late are shifts.

4.4 The Reliever Chef is required for the pilot measures, but is not a real post yet

Section 2 requires a Reliever who can cover either main chef shift. Section 10 requires off-days without excessive hours. Section 11 then asks after four weeks whether the Reliever should be external or developed internally. If the Reliever is only a hope for week 5, the week 1–4 resilience measure fails by design and I will cover the off-days again.

4.5 KA/KP headcount is three different ideas at once

The script offers three full-time posts, a “confirm actual hours” hold, and a float principle. Relief/Float is both a window and a post. KA and KP are never defined — Kitchen Assistant and Kitchen Porter are different skills. There is no 7-day worked example, so nobody can see whether three posts exist to cover the week or to stand together on the same Tuesday. At 20 guests, do not open as if the house were 35 covers.

4.6 The four-hour chef overlap is justified for 30–35 covers, not for 20

07:00–16:00 and 12:00–21:00 give 18 chef-hours a day. At 20 guests, two chefs both producing lunch for four hours idle skilled time and contradict “better use of skilled hours”. Keep the clock times if they fit breakfast and dinner. Write the overlap rule: one chef owns lunch; the other uses the block for handover, quality, tomorrow’s prep and Head Chef planning.

4.7 Head Chef planning time is hoped for, not rostered

“Where practical” and a weekly admin day that “may be used where agreed” are how planning time disappears. Roster a protected block with named outputs: 7-day plan, 48-hour confirmation, order list, support requirement. Give us the template in week one.

4.8 Ownership leaks will put Restaurant work back onto Kitchen

  • Fruit. Same fruit, same knife, same fridge unless there is a labelled par and a named cutter.
  • Restaurant wash-up. “When directed and capacity allows” is how temporary help becomes the daily method.
  • “When Kitchen work is complete.” The chef leading the shift must say so against a close checklist.
  • Shared scullery / dishwasher. Say who owns it at 15:00 and at 21:00.
  • FOH milk versus Kitchen dairy. Separate par, or it returns to my list.

4.9 Duty Manager versus Head Chef is not tight enough for live service

“Genuine” food-safety requirements are not defined. There is no live RACI for the pass, a delayed service or an 86’d dish. “No separate Duty Manager role” reads as if Duty Manager work has been removed. It has not: Shannon and Louise are the Duty Managers; no third DM post is proposed. Until the GM decides a deadlock, the food-safety position stands.

4.10 Ordering and stock is a principle, not a system

“Agreed list/system” and “2–3 authorised people” are unnamed. There are no par levels, order cut-offs, supplier list, food-cost target or waste record. Name the list and the authorised orderers, and train them before the pilot.

4.11 Pilot measures are not measurable

Measure in the script Problem
Ready for planned service time Service times are not in the paper. No late-minute log.
Agreed food quality Agreed with whom, scored how?
No labour reduction compromises safety A constraint, not a measure.
Off-days without excessive hours “Excessive” is undefined. No overtime cap or TOIL rule.
Chefs not “routinely” used for washing “Routinely” is undefined.
Extra hour has a recorded reason Where, and who writes it?
Plans are “clear enough” No template, so it cannot be audited.

Also missing: allergen/incident log, waste, food-cost movement, overtime hours, on-time service count, and a week-2 review. Four weeks is long enough to embed a wrong rota.

4.12 The decision section has no pass mark

Adopt / adjust / confirm Reliever / confirm KA mix is a process, not a standard. Agree numbers before day one: for example on-time handovers, zero safety incidents, and a maximum number of extra hours at 20 guests.

4.13 The script is missing the work that employment documents must contain

  • Breakfast, lunch and dinner service times.
  • Menu style and how guest diets reach the Kitchen.
  • Opening and closing checklists.
  • A worked 7-day rota at 20 guests showing off-days and the close-to-open rule.
  • Break entitlement on 8- and 9-hour shifts.
  • Working Time rest: 21:00 to 07:00 is 10 hours, not 11. The forbid rule is right; it is a legal rest issue.
  • Pay, overtime or TOIL for recorded extra hours.
  • Induction and competency sign-off.
  • Order cut-off and delivery days.
  • What happens if a week jumps from 20 toward 30 guests.

4.14 Language that will cause arguments later

KA/KP is used as one job; it is two. The “original pilot model” is mentioned and not attached. Late 12:00–21:00 and morning 07:00 cannot be judged without service times. Section 6’s “no separate Duty Manager role” will be misread as the removal of Duty Manager work.

5. What I recommend we issue instead

  1. Reissue the proposal at 20 guests. Delete 30–35 from purpose, shift titles and the measures table.
  2. Attach the full work descriptions as the employment attachment.
  3. Name the Reliever and the authorised orderers before week 1.
  4. Roster the overlap as handover and Head Chef planning, not as two chefs producing 20 lunches.
  5. Roster support as two windows plus relief, and let the 20-guest hours decide whether three full-time KA/KP posts are justified.
  6. Issue a one-page scorecard and a week-2 review, then decide at week 4 against agreed numbers.
  7. Keep the principle that is already right: Kitchen owns the food and the technical Kitchen standard. Restaurant owns guest-facing dining and refreshment. I define the Kitchen requirement. Shannon and Louise coordinate live deployment. The GM decides a deadlock.

I am ready to run the 4-week pilot on that basis.
