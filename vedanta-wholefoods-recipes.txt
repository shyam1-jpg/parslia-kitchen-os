Method

Air Fryer Corn with Paprika and Peanuts
──────────────────────────────────────────────────

Air Fryer Corn with Paprika and Peanuts is a sattvic vegetarian dish of fresh corn cobs, organic extra virgin olive oil, organic paprika. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Course: Main Course  Prep: 5 min  Cook: 15 min  Yield: 4 people  Diet: vegetarian, sattvic, no onion/garlic, egg-free

Ingredients
• 4 fresh corn cobs
• 2 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
• 1 tsp organic paprika
• natural fine sea salt
• organic cracked black pepper
• 2 tbsp water
For the garnish:
• Lime wedges
• Chopped coriander
• Sliced red chillies
• roasted peanuts (crushed)

Method
1. Line the air fryer basket with parchment paper made for air fryers.
2. Pour a small splash of water (about 2 tbsp) onto the parchment to create a touch of steam and keep the corn juicy.
3. Brush the corn evenly with olive oil, making sure all the kernels get a good coating.
4. Sprinkle over the paprika, then season generously with salt and pepper.
5. Arrange the cobs in a single layer on top of the parchment.
6. Air fry at 200°C for 12 minutes, turning once halfway through, until the corn is lightly charred and golden in spots.
7. Serve hot straight from the fryer with a squeeze of lime, a scattering of coriander, fresh sliced red chillies, and a generous sprinkle of crushed roasted peanuts.

Kitchen notes
— Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
— Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.

Almond Pesto With Roasted Tomato
──────────────────────────────────────────────────

Almond Pesto With Roasted Tomato is a sattvic vegetarian dish of organic extra virgin olive oil, cherry tomatoes, organic spaghetti. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Course: Main Course  Prep: 15 min  Cook: 15 min  Yield: 4 People  Diet: vegetarian, sattvic, no onion/garlic, egg-free

Ingredients
• ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
• 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
• 500 g cherry tomatoes
• 400 g organic spaghetti
• Natural fine sea salt
• organic cracked black pepper
For the pesto:
• 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
• 50 g organic almonds
• 100 g vegetarian hard cheese or nutritional yeast or plant-based cheese
• handful of basil
• 1/2 lemon
• natural fine sea salt

Method
1. To make the pesto, place the almonds in a frying pan and roast over a medium to high heat until they start to colour.
2. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
3. This should take 3-4 minutes, then remove from the pan and allow to cool.
4. Roughly chop the ginger and juice the lemon then place in a food processor with the almonds and the rest of the pesto ingredients.
5. Blitz until roughly chopped but not completely smooth.
6. Next, place the tomatoes in a baking tray, drizzle with the olive oil and season generously with salt and pepper.
7. Roast in the oven for 20 minutes at 200c then set aside in a warm place.
8. Cook the spaghetti according to packet instructions, drain (reserving 2 tbsp of the cooking water) then add back to the saucepan.
9. Mix in the pesto along with the cooking water and serve with the roasted tomatoes on top.

Kitchen notes
— Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
— Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.
— Hing is bloomed in hot ghee or oil; a pinch is enough.
Allergens (kitchen estimate): gluten

Apple and Pecan Tart
──────────────────────────────────────────────────

Apple and Pecan Tart is an egg-free vegetarian sweet for a Vedanta kitchen — built around apples, butter or plant based alternative, organic brown sugar. Offer it fresh, without onion, garlic or eggs.

Course: Dessert  Prep: 15 min  Cook: 30 min  Yield: 4  Diet: vegetarian, sattvic, no onion/garlic, egg-free

Ingredients
• 2 apples
• 50 g butter or plant based alternative
• 1 tbsp organic brown sugar
• 1 tbsp organic maple syrup
• 1 tsp organic vanilla extract
• 1 tsp organic plain flour
• 1 large sheet of ready rolled puff pastry
• 50 g organic pecans

Method
1. Peel 2 apples, slice into quarters, removed the core then finely slice.
2. Place the apples in a frying pan with the butter, sugar and maple syrup.
3. Cook on a medium heat for 2-3 minutes, stirring gently so that the apples don’t break up.
4. Add the vanilla extract and flour and cook for a further 2-3 minutes, again stirring gently.
5. Set aside and let cool.
6. Now place the pecans in a frying pan and gently toast until they become golden.
7. Allow to cool, then roughly chop.
8. Next, take the sheet of pastry and lay on a piece of baking paper.
9. Place a plate roughly the same size as the pastry on top and cut around to create a circle.
10. Take a sharp knife and lightly cut a smaller circle about 1cm from the edge. Don’t cut right through the pastry, just make an indent.
11. Now neatly lay the cooled apples on top of the pastry.
12. Bake at 200c for 20 minutes or until the pastry is golden brown.
13. Scatter the chopped pecans on top and serve warm or allow to cool.

Kitchen notes
— Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
— Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.
Allergens (kitchen estimate): gluten, dairy

• 4 radishes finely sliced
• Half a cucumber diced in 1cm cubes

• Handful of chopped coriander
For the dressing:

• 1 lime juiced

5. Combine all ingredients together then pour over the dressing.

Aubergine and Harissa Rice
──────────────────────────────────────────────────

Aubergine and Harissa Rice is a sattvic vegetarian dish of organic extra virgin olive oil, celery stalk, aubergine. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Course: Lunch, Main Course  Prep: 15 min  Cook: 55 min  Yield: 4 people  Diet: vegetarian, sattvic, no onion/garlic, egg-free

Ingredients
• ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
• 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
• 1 celery stalk
• 250 g aubergine, diced
• 25 g sun-dried tomatoes, plus 30 g walnuts, chopped
• 2 bay leaves
• 2 tsp harissa paste
• 350 g organic brown rice
• 700 ml vegetable stock
• natural fine sea salt
• organic cracked black pepper

Method
1. To start, place the soaked sun-dried tomatoes in a bowl and cover with hot water for 5 minutes, drain and roughly chop.
2. Finely chop the celery, celery and ginger and roughly chop the aubergine.
3. Add all of these ingredients to a saucepan with the olive oil and bay leaf, season with salt and pepper and cook on a medium heat for 20 minutes stirring regularly.
4. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
5. Add the harissa and cook for 2 minutes.
6. Add the rice and cook for another 2 minutes.
7. Add the vegetable stock, bring to the boil then turn down and simmer for 25-30 minutes or until the rice is cooked.
8. As an optional extra top with toasted pine nuts and fresh parsley.

Kitchen notes
— Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
— Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.
— Hing is bloomed in hot ghee or oil; a pinch is enough.
— Aubergine, walnuts and sun-dried tomato stand in for mushrooms.

Aubergine Pappardelle
──────────────────────────────────────────────────

Aubergine Pappardelle is a sattvic vegetarian dish of aubergine, organic thyme, sun-dried tomatoes. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Course: dinner, Main Course  Prep: 15 min  Cook: 40 min  Yield: 4 people  Diet: vegetarian, sattvic, no onion/garlic, egg-free

Ingredients
• ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
• 250 g aubergine, cut into 1 cm slices
• 250 g aubergine, finely diced
• 1 tsp organic thyme
• 10 g sun-dried tomatoes, plus 30 g walnuts, chopped
• 2 tsp organic fresh lemon juice
• 1/2 lemon
• 400 g pappardelle
• 100 g vegetarian hard cheese or nutritional yeast
• 2 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
• natural fine sea salt
• organic cracked black pepper

Method
1. Cut the sliced aubergine into approximately 1cm slices and lay in a baking tray.
2. Drizzle over 1 tbsp of the olive oil, add 1/2 tsp of thyme and season with salt and pepper.
3. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
4. Roast at 200c for 20 minutes, remove from the oven and set aside in a warm place.
5. Place the soaked sun-dried tomatoes in a bowl, cover with hot water and leave to soak for 5 minutes. Strain and finely chop.
6. Finely dice the fennel or celery and add to a saucepan with 1 tbsp of olive oil and fry on a medium heat for 2 minutes.
7. Grate a little ginger and add to the pan along with the soaked sun-dried tomatoes and 1/2 tsp of thyme, cook for another 2 minutes.
8. Add the lemon juice and reduce for 1 minute.
9. Finely chop the diced aubergine and add to the pan, season with salt and pepper and cook for 10 minutes or until the aubergine is golden.
10. Cook the pasta according to packet instructions and add to the aubergine along with the roasted sliced aubergine.
11. Add the cheese, a squeeze of lemon and garnish with some chopped coriander.

Kitchen notes
— Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
— Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.
— Hing is bloomed in hot ghee or oil; a pinch is enough.
— Aubergine, walnuts and sun-dried tomato stand in for mushrooms.
Allergens (kitchen estimate): dairy

• 1 carrot
• 2 celery stalks
• 1 bay leaf
• 1/2 tsp rosemary, chopped
• 250 g potato
• 250 g organic butter beans (cooked)
• 1 litre vegetable stock
• 150 ml cashew cream or oat cream
• large handful of parsley

Cauliflower Cheese Soup is a sattvic vegetarian dish of celery stalks, organic thyme, cauliflower. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Spicy Lentil Stew is a sattvic vegetarian dish of organic extra virgin olive oil, medium carrots medium diced, sun-dried tomatoes. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Course: dinner, Lunch, Main Course, Side Dish, Soup  Prep: 15 min  Cook: 50 min  Yield: 6 people  Diet: vegetarian, sattvic, no onion/garlic, egg-free

Ingredients
• ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
• 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
• 2 medium carrots medium diced
• 30 g sun-dried tomatoes, plus 30 g walnuts, chopped
• 250 g aubergine, diced
• 1 organic coconut milk can
• 1 litre organic vegetable stock
• 250 g organic french type lentils
• 200 g chopped kale
• natural sea salt crystals
• organic cracked black pepper
For the paste:
• 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
• 1 medium thumb ginger skin removed
• 1 dsp tomato puree
• 1 tsp organic cumin
• 1 tsp organic coriander
• 1 tsp organic turmeric
• 50 ml water

Method
1. Blend all of the paste ingredients together and fry in a pan on a medium heat for 5 minutes. Remove the cooked paste, set aside and clean the pan out.
2. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
3. Add the oil and fry the carrots, soaked sun-dried tomatoes, aubergine, season generously with salt and pepper and fry for 20 minutes.
4. Now add back in the cooked paste, coconut milk, lentils and vegetable stock and simmer for 40 minutes.
5. Finally add the lentils and kale and cook for a final 5 minutes and you are ready to serve.

Kitchen notes
— Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
— Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.
— Hing is bloomed in hot ghee or oil; a pinch is enough.
— Aubergine, walnuts and sun-dried tomato stand in for mushrooms.

Spicy Plant-Based Ragu
──────────────────────────────────────────────────

Spicy Plant-Based Ragu is a sattvic vegetarian dish of organic extra virgin olive oil, organic cooked brown lentils, organic chilli flakes. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Course: dinner, Lunch, Main Course  Prep: 5 min  Cook: 40 min  Yield: 4 People  Diet: vegetarian, sattvic, no onion/garlic, egg-free

Ingredients
• ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
• 2 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
• 400 g organic cooked brown lentils, drained
• 1/2 tsp organic chilli flakes
• 1/2 tsp organic fennel seeds
• 1 organic tinned tomatoes
• 1 can of water
• A large handful of basil
• 50 g plant-based cheese
• 400 g organic penne
• natural fine sea salt
• organic cracked black pepper

Method
1. In a large saucepan, add 1 tbsp of olive oil along with the cooked lentils.
2. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
3. Fry on a medium to high heat for 5 minutes or until golden brown.
4. Remove from the pan and set aside.
5. Finally chop the ginger and add to the same pan along with another tbsp of olive oil.
6. Add the chilli flakes and fennel seeds and cook on a medium heat for 2 minutes.
7. Next add the can of tomatoes along with a can with of water.
8. Bring to the boil then turn down and simmer for 30 minutes.
9. Cook your pasta according to the packet instructions, drain then add to the tomato sauce.
10. Add back in the brown the cooked lentils and cook for 2 minutes.
11. Roughly tear the basil, grate the cheese and add both to the pot.
12. Mix well and serve.

Kitchen notes
— Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
— Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.

• 2 tsp paprika
• 2 tbsp olive oil

• natural fine sea salt

Dressing

Ingredients
• ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
• 4 baking potatoes
• 400 ml organic coconut milk
• 1 cup frozen peas
• 2 cups fresh spinach
• natural fine sea salt, to taste
Organic Masala Paste
• 1 tsp organic fenugreek
• 1 tbsp organic coriander
• 1 tbsp chilli powder
• 1 tbsp organic garam masala
• 1 tbsp organic turmeric
• 2 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
• 2 inches fresh ginger

Method
1. Make a cross on the potatoes and bake them at 180 degrees, until nearly done, switch off the oven and keep the potatoes inside.
2. Combine the fenugreek, coriander, chilli powder, garam masala, turmeric, olive oil and fresh ginger in a blender and combine to make a paste.
3. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
4. Heat up some oil in a heavy bottom duty pan.
5. Sweat the celery until soft, add the curry paste and fry for one minute.
6. Add coconut milk and bring to a gentle boil, stirring occasionally.
7. Add frozen peas, spinach and taste for salt.
8. Transfer the curry to a deep tray, place the potatoes in the middle. bake for another 10 minutes until potatoes are soft and fluffy.
9. Before serving, squeeze each potato from the bottom so the cross opens up, place indian pickles inside, grab a big spoon to pour the curry over and enjoy!

Tarka Dhal is a sattvic vegetarian dish of organic red split lentils, large thumb of ginger grated, organic extra virgin olive oil. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Course: Lunch, Main Course  Prep: 5 min  Cook: 60 min  Yield: 4 People  Diet: vegetarian, sattvic, no onion/garlic, egg-free

Ingredients
• ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
• 250 g organic red split lentils
• 1 large thumb of ginger grated
• 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
• 1 medium red chilli finely chopped
• 2 tbsp organic granules
• 1 tsp organic yellow mustard seeds
• 1 tsp organic ground coriander
• 1 tsp organic ground cumin
• 1 tsp organic ground turmeric
• A handful of fresh coriander
• 500 ml water
• fine sea salt

Method
1. Wash the lentils then place in a large saucepan with the water and grated ginger.
2. Bring to the boil then reduce to a simmer. Remove any scum that comes to the surface.
3. Cook for 1 hour (stirring regularly) or until the lentils soften. You may need to add more water. Season generously with salt.
4. Next put the oil in a frying pan and fry the mustard seeds until they start to pop open.
5. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
6. Then add the celery and cook for 2 minutes until it starts to soften.
7. Next add the ginger and chilli and cook for a further 2 minutes.
8. Now add the coriander, cumin, turmeric and tomato paste and cook gently for 5 minutes taking care to not burn the spices.
9. Finally, empty the pan into the pot of cooked lentils and stir throughly.
10. Serve topped with chopped fresh coriander.

Vegetable Paella is a sattvic vegetarian dish of organic extra virgin olive oil, celery stalks finely diced, medium carrot finely diced. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Course: dinner, Lunch  Prep: 10 min  Cook: 40 min  Yield: 6 people  Diet: vegetarian, sattvic, no onion/garlic, egg-free

Ingredients
• ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
• 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
• 2 celery stalks finely diced
• 1 medium carrot finely diced
• 2 thyme sprigs
• 1 bay leaf
• 1 dsp tomato puree
• 2 tsp organic paprika
• 1 red pepper diced
• 250 g organic paella rice
• pinch of saffron
• 700 ml organic vegetable stock
• 150 g tomato roughly chopped
• 100 g peas
• natural sea salt crystals
• organic cracked black pepper

Method
1. Fry the celery, celery and carrot in olive oil and season with salt and pepper. Cook on a medium heat for 10 minutes stirring frequently.
2. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
3. Add the thyme, bay leaf and ginger and cook for 2 minutes.
4. Next add the tomato puree, paprika and red pepper and cook for a further 2 minutes.
5. Now add the rice and stir into the vegetables.
6. Add the saffron and vegetable stock and cook for 20 minutes.
7. Finally add the peas and cook for last 5 minutes and serve.

Kitchen notes
— Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
— Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.
— Hing is bloomed in hot ghee or oil; a pinch is enough.

Vegetable, Ginger and Lime Noodles
──────────────────────────────────────────────────

Vegetable, Ginger and Lime Noodles is a sattvic vegetarian dish of thumb of ginger, lime, organic coconut aminos. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Course: dinner, Lunch, Main Course  Prep: 10 min  Cook: 10 min  Yield: 4 People  Diet: vegetarian, sattvic, no onion/garlic, egg-free

Ingredients
• ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
• 1 thumb of ginger
• 1 lime
• 1 tbsp organic coconut aminos
• 1 tbsp organic toasted sesame oil
• 400 g broccoli stems
• A large handful of spinach
• 250 g organic soba noodles

Method
1. To make the sauce, peel the ginger and finely grate. Do the same with the ginger.
2. Juice the lime.
3. Add all to a small bowl along with the soy sauce and sesame oil. Whisk together and set aside.
4. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
5. Chop the broccoli stems into approximately 2cm pieces and boil in salted water for 2 minutes.
6. Drain and set aside.
7. Cook the noodles according to packet instructions, drain and then add back to the pan.
8. Pour the sauce over the noodles and add the broccoli and spinach.
9. Cook on a medium heat for 2 minutes and then serve.

Kitchen notes
— Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
— Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.
— Hing is bloomed in hot ghee or oil; a pinch is enough.
Allergens (kitchen estimate): sesame

Walnut and Aubergine Ragu
──────────────────────────────────────────────────

Walnut and Aubergine Ragu is a sattvic vegetarian dish of organic chilli flakes, tins of tomatoes, tin of water. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Course: dinner  Prep: 15 min  Cook: 55 min  Yield: 4 People  Diet: vegetarian, sattvic, no onion/garlic, egg-free

Ingredients
• ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
• 1/2 tsp organic chilli flakes
• 2 tins of tomatoes
• 1/2 tin of water
• 2 tsp organic tomato puree
• 5 g sun-dried tomatoes, plus 30 g walnuts, chopped
• 500 g aubergine, diced
• 1 tbsp organic pine nuts
• A handful of parsley
• 400 g organic penne pasta
• 2 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
• natural fine sea salt
• organic cracked black pepper

Method
1. Firstly make the tomato sauce.
2. Grate a little ginger and add to a saucepan with the chilli flakes and olive oil.
3. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
4. Fry on a medium heat for 2 minutes so that the ginger colours.
5. Add the tinned tomatoes along with half a tin of water, the tomato puree and salt and pepper.
6. Simmer on a medium heat for about half an hour, stirring occasionally until the sauce thickens.
7. Next, soak the soaked sun-dried tomatoes in warm water for 10 mins, then drain and finely chop.
8. Roughly slice the aubergine and add to a frying pan with the soaked sun-dried tomatoes and 1 tbsp of olive oil.
9. Cook on a medium heat for 10 minutes.
10. Finely chop the celery and add to the pan and season generously.
11. Cook for a further 10 minutes stirring frequently.
12. Roughly chop the parsley and add to the aubergine along with the pine nuts.
13. Mix together and remove from the heat.
14. Cook your pasta according to the packet instructions and drain well.
15. Mix your cooked pasta with the tomato sauce, serve and spoon the aubergine ragu on top.

Kitchen notes
— Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
— Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.
— Hing is bloomed in hot ghee or oil; a pinch is enough.
— Aubergine, walnuts and sun-dried tomato stand in for mushrooms.
Allergens (kitchen estimate): gluten

Walnut Pesto and Peas
──────────────────────────────────────────────────

Walnut Pesto and Peas is a sattvic salad of organic wholewheat spaghetti pasta, peas, organic extra virgin olive oil. Alliums are left out; lemon, ginger and roasted cumin carry the dressing.

Course: Appetizer, Lunch, Main Course, Salad, Side Dish, Snack  Prep: 5 min  Cook: 10 min  Yield: 4 people  Diet: vegetarian, sattvic, no onion/garlic, egg-free

Ingredients
• 400 g organic wholewheat spaghetti pasta
• 150 g peas
• 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
• Salt and pepper
Pesto
• 2 tbsp organic lime juice
• A large handful of basil
• A large handful of parsley
• 50 g organic walnut pieces
• 2 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
• Salt and pepper
• ½ tsp roasted cumin powder
• ½ tsp fresh ginger, grated

Method
1. For the pesto, place all of the ingredients except the olive oil in a food processor and blend until smooth. Next slowly drizzle in the olive oil whilst the processor is running.
2. Cook the spaghetti as per the cooking instructions, adding the peas to the pot 2 minutes before the end of the cooking time.
3. Next, heat 1 tbsp olive oil in a pan and add the pesto. Heat through gently before adding the strained spaghetti and peas.
4. Mix well, season with salt and pepper and serve with an Italian hard cheese such as vegetarian hard cheese or nutritional yeast.

Kitchen notes
— Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
— Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.
— Cold dressings use lemon, ginger and roasted cumin instead of garlic.
Allergens (kitchen estimate): gluten, nuts

Walnut Tempeh Burger
──────────────────────────────────────────────────

Walnut Tempeh Burger is a sattvic vegetarian dish of tomato puree mixed with chilli powder and a little maple, soy sauce, organic sesame oil. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Course: dinner, Lunch, Main Course  Prep: 40 min  Cook: 5 min  Yield: 4 People  Diet: vegetarian, sattvic, no onion/garlic, egg-free

Ingredients
• 1 tbsp tomato puree mixed with chilli powder and a little maple
• 1 tbsp soy sauce
• 1 tbsp organic sesame oil
• 200 g organic tempeh
• 2 aubergine, cut into 1 cm slices
• organic chilli sauce
• 2 organic bread rolls
• 80 g walnuts, finely chopped

Method
1. Firstly put the chilli-tomato paste, soy sauce and sesame oil in a bowl and mix together.
2. Cut the tempeh into approximately 0.5cm slices, add to the bowl and coat with the chilli-tomato paste mix.
3. Now cut the aubergine into 1cm slices and coat with the chilli-tomato paste as well.
4. Leave to marinade for half an hour.
5. Take a large frying pan and cook the tempeh and aubergine on a high heat for 5 minutes turning occasionally.
6. Cut the rolls in half and add some of the sriracha.
7. Divide the tempeh and aubergine between the rolls and top with your favourite salad.

Kitchen notes
— Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
— Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.
— Aubergine, walnuts and sun-dried tomato stand in for mushrooms.
Allergens (kitchen estimate): gluten, sesame, soya

Walnut-Bean Kofta with Tomato Sauce
──────────────────────────────────────────────────

Walnut-Bean Kofta with Tomato Sauce is a sattvic vegetarian dish of organic extra virgin olive oil, aubergine, organic thyme. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Course: dinner, Lunch, Main Course  Prep: 15 min  Cook: 70 min  Yield: 6 people  Diet: vegetarian, sattvic, no onion/garlic, egg-free

Ingredients
• ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
• 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
• 300 g aubergine, diced
• 1 tsp organic thyme
• 1 dsp tomato puree
• 1 tsp organic paprika
• 100 g organic jasmine brown rice
• 200 g organic turtle beans cooked
• 100 g vegetarian hard cheese or nutritional yeast
• 100 g organic breadcrumbs
• Large handful of parsley
• 1 tsp ground flaxseed
• Salt & pepper
• 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
• 2 organic tomato tins
• Large handful of basil
• Salt & pepper
• Plus 600g organic spaghetti
• 80 g walnuts, finely chopped

Method
1. Make a simple tomato sauce by frying the chopped ginger in olive oil until it takes on a bit of colour.
2. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
3. Next add the tins of tomatoes, salt and pepper. Bring to the boil then turn down to a simmer and cook for 30 minutes stirring frequently. Finally add the basil and set aside.
4. To make the kofta, fry the fennel or celery, aubergine, ginger and thyme gently in olive oil for 15 minutes.
5. Season with salt and pepper then add the tomato puree and paprika. Cook for a further 5 mins, remove the mixture and let cool.
6. Place the cooked and drained turtle beans in the same pan and fry gently for 5 minutes or until any moisture has evaporated. Remove the beans and let cool.
7. Make a flax egg by mixing 1 tsp of flaxseed with 3 tbsp of warm water, then let sit for 5 minutes.
8. Place the aubergine mixture, flax egg and beans in a food processor, then add the brown rice, toasted breadcrumbs, cheese and parsley. Blend until the mixture takes on a ‘stuffing’ like consistency.
9. Roll the mixture out into golf-ball sized balls and place on a baking tray lined with baking paper. Bake in an oven at 200c for 10 minutes.
10. Cook the spaghetti according to packet instructions and then combine with half of the tomato sauce.
11. Place a portion of spaghetti on each plate, top with kofta, then spoon over some of the remaining tomato sauce.
12. Top with vegetarian hard cheese or nutritional yeast and serve.

Kitchen notes
— Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
— Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.
— Hing is bloomed in hot ghee or oil; a pinch is enough.
— Aubergine, walnuts and sun-dried tomato stand in for mushrooms.
Allergens (kitchen estimate): gluten, dairy
