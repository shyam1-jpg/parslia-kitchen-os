# Spicy Satay Noodles

Spicy Satay Noodles is a sattvic vegetarian dish of organic extra virgin olive oil, tenderstem broccoli cut into 3cm long pieces, sugar snap peas. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

- **Diet:** vegetarian, sattvic, no onion, no garlic, egg-free
- **Course:** Appetizer, Lunch, Main Course, Side Dish, Snack
- **Cuisine:** American, Chinese, French, Indian, Italian, Japanese, Mediterranean, Mexican
- **Prep:** 10 min
- **Cook:** 10 min
- **Yield:** 4 people
- **Allergens (estimate):** gluten, nuts, soya

## Ingredients

- ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
- 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
- 100 g tenderstem broccoli cut into 3cm long pieces
- 100 g sugar snap peas
- 250 g organic wheat asia noodles
- 1 tbsp organic tamari soya sauce

### Satay Sauce

- 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
- 1 tbsp organic chilli flakes
- 1 thumb ginger finely chopped
- 400 ml organic coconut milk
- 1 tsp organic coconut palm sugar
- 1 tbsp organic tamari soya sauce
- 1 tbsp organic peanut butter
- 2 tbsp organic lime juice

## Method

1. To make the satay sauce, fry the chilli, ginger, ginger and fennel or celery in oil for 3 minutes.
2. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
3. Add the coconut milk, coconut palm sugar, lime juice, tamari soya sauce and peanut butter. Gently heat for 5 minutes taking care to not boil the coconut milk so that it doesn’t split.
4. Blend with a stick blender and put aside.
5. Cook the noodles according to the packet instructions.
6. In a wok, fry the broccoli and sugar snap peas in oil for 2 minutes before adding the tamari soy sauce. Add the cooked noodles and a few tablespoons of the satay sauce.
7. Serve topped with coriander stems, a wedge of lime with the remainder of the sauce on the side.

## Vedanta kitchen notes

- Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
- Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.
- Hing is bloomed in hot ghee or oil; a pinch is enough.
