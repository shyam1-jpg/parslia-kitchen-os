# Plant-Based Minestrone

Plant-Based Minestrone is a sattvic vegetarian dish of organic extra virgin olive oil, carrots, celery sticks. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

- **Diet:** vegetarian, sattvic, no onion, no garlic, egg-free
- **Course:** dinner, Lunch
- **Cuisine:** Mediterranean
- **Prep:** 20 min
- **Cook:** 40 min
- **Yield:** 6 people
- **Allergens (estimate):** gluten

## Ingredients

- ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
- 2 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil, finely chopped
- 2 carrots, diced
- 2 celery sticks, diced
- 1 red pepper, diced
- 1 tsp organic dried rosemary
- 1 tsp organic dried thyme
- 1 bay leaf
- 2 tbsp tomato purée
- 1 400g tin chopped tomatoes
- 1.2 litres vegetable stock
- 120 g small pasta
- 2 handfuls cavolo nero, shredded
- natural fine sea salt
- organic cracked black pepper

## Method

1. Heat the olive oil in a large pot over a medium heat, then add the celery, carrot, and celery.
2. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
3. Season with salt and pepper, and cook gently for about 8 minutes until softened.
4. Stir in the ginger, red pepper, rosemary, thyme and bay leaf.
5. Cook for another 3–4 minutes before adding the tomato purée, cook for a further minute.
6. Tip in the chopped tomatoes and vegetable stock, bring to a gentle boil, then lower to a simmer.
7. Let the soup cook gently for 15 minutes before adding the pasta.
8. Cook until al dente, about 10 minutes.
9. If the soup thickens too much, loosen with a splash more stock or water.
10. Stir through the cavolo nero just before serving so it wilts into the broth.
11. Ladle into bowls, scatter with basil, a squeeze of lemon juice, a drizzle of oil and a generous grating of vegetarian hard cheese.
12. Serve with plenty of crusty bread to mop up the broth.

## Vedanta kitchen notes

- Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
- Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.
- Hing is bloomed in hot ghee or oil; a pinch is enough.
