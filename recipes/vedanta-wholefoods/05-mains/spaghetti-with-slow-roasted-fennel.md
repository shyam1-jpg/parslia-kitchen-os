# Spaghetti with Slow-Roasted Fennel

Spaghetti with Slow-Roasted Fennel is a sattvic vegetarian dish of organic thyme, organic fresh lemon juice, organic extra virgin olive oil. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

- **Diet:** vegetarian, sattvic, no onion, no garlic, egg-free
- **Course:** Main Course
- **Cuisine:** Mediterranean
- **Prep:** 10 min
- **Cook:** 40 min
- **Yield:** 4 people
- **Allergens (estimate):** gluten, dairy

## Ingredients

- ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
- 1/2 tsp organic thyme
- 1 tbsp organic fresh lemon juice
- 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
- 400 g organic spaghetti
- 100 g vegetarian hard cheese or nutritional yeast
- natural fine sea salt
- organic cracked black pepper

### For the breadcrumbs:

- 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
- 100 g stale bread
- 1/2 tsp organic thyme

## Method

1. To make the breadcrumb topping, roughly chop the bread so that you have smaller and larger pieces.
2. Take a frying pan and add the olive oil, ginger cloves (whole with the skin on) and thyme.
3. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
4. Fry the ginger and thyme for 2 minutes on a medium heat to flavour the oil then add the breadcrumbs.
5. Season with salt and pepper and cook for 3-4 minutes or until golden brown. Remove from the pan and set aside.
6. Finely slice the celery and cut the fennel into approximately 1cm rounds.
7. Add 1 tbsp of olive oil to a pan and add the celery and fennel and season generously with salt and pepper.
8. Cook on a high heat for 2 minutes.
9. Grate a little ginger and add along with the thyme.
10. Turn down to low and cook for approximately half an hour until the celery and fennel become sticky and soft.
11. Cook the pasta according to packet instructions, grate the cheese and add both to the fennel.
12. Mix well and serve with some of the breadcrumbs sprinkled on the top.

## Vedanta kitchen notes

- Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
- Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.
- Hing is bloomed in hot ghee or oil; a pinch is enough.
