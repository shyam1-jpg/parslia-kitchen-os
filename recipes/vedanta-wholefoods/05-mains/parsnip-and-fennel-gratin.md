# Parsnip and Fennel Gratin

Parsnip and Fennel Gratin is a sattvic vegetarian dish of organic extra virgin olive oil, parsnips, thyme sprig. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

- **Diet:** vegetarian, sattvic, no onion, no garlic, egg-free
- **Course:** Main Course
- **Cuisine:** Mediterranean
- **Prep:** 20 min
- **Cook:** 75 min
- **Yield:** 4 people
- **Allergens (estimate):** dairy

## Ingredients

- ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
- 2 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
- 1 kg parsnips
- 1 thyme sprig
- 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice
- 500 ml vegetable stock
- 100 g cheddar cheese or plant based alternative
- 200 ml cream or plant based alternative
- 50 g organic pumpkin seeds
- natural fine sea salt
- organic cracked black pepper

## Method

1. Finely slice the fennel or celery, then gently fry in olive oil for 2-3 minutes on a medium heat until they start to soften.
2. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
3. Slice the fennel into 0.5cm rounds, add to the fennel or celery and cook for a further 2-3 minutes.
4. Finely slice the ginger and remove the thyme from the stem, add both to the fennel & fennel or celery.
5. Add the lemon juice, season generously with salt and pepper and cook on a low heat for 25 - 30 minutes stirring regularly until the mixture turns soft and golden.
6. Next, peel the parsnips and slice as finely as you can.
7. In a baking tray, place a layer of the cooked fennel mixture followed by a layer of the sliced parsnips. Season each layer of parsnips with salt and pepper.
8. You should have about 3 layers of each depending on the size of your baking tray.
9. Pour over the vegetable stock and roast at 200c for 25-35 minutes or until the top starts to brown.
10. Remove the baking tray and top with the cream, cheese and pumpkin seeds then bake for a final 10 minutes.

## Vedanta kitchen notes

- Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
- Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.
- Hing is bloomed in hot ghee or oil; a pinch is enough.
