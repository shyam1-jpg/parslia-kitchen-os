# Cauliflower Katsu

Cauliflower Katsu is a sattvic vegetarian dish of large cauliflower, cooked chickpeas, ’s rapeseed or vegetable oil. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

- **Diet:** vegetarian, sattvic, no onion, no garlic, egg-free
- **Course:** dinner, Lunch
- **Cuisine:** world
- **Prep:** 10 min
- **Cook:** 60 min
- **Yield:** 4 People
- **Allergens (estimate):** gluten, soya

## Ingredients

- ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
- 1 large cauliflower
- 250 g cooked chickpeas
- 2 tbsp’s rapeseed or vegetable oil
- 1 large thumb of ginger peeled and grated
- 1 tbsp organic turmeric
- 1 tbsp curry powder
- 300 ml organic vegetable bouillon
- 200 ml organic coconut milk
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tsp organic brown sugar
- natural fine sea salt
- organic cracked black pepper

## Method

1. Cut the cauliflower into approximately 3cm pieces, place into a baking tray and drizzle with 1 tbsp of oil and season with salt and pepper.
2. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
3. Place in an oven at 220c and roast for 25 minutes or until golden brown (turning over the cauliflower every 5 minutes).
4. Add the chickpeas and roast for a further 5 minutes.
5. To make the katsu sauce, fry the celery in 1 tbsp of vegetable oil on a medium heat for 2 minutes.
6. Add the ginger and ginger and cook for a further 2 minutes.
7. Next add the turmeric and curry powder and cook for 1 minute.
8. Add the vegetable stock, bring to the boil and simmer gently for 25 minutes.
9. Now add the coconut milk, soy sauce and sugar.
10. Blitz with a stick blender and gently heat for 5 minutes taking care to not boil the sauce so the coconut milk doesn’t curdle.
11. Serve your roasted cauliflower and chickpeas with rice and drizzle with the Katsu sauce.

## Vedanta kitchen notes

- Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
- Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.
- Hing is bloomed in hot ghee or oil; a pinch is enough.
