# Roasted Vegetable and Couscous Salad

Roasted Vegetable and Couscous Salad is a sattvic salad of small cauliflower, carrots, organic extra virgin olive oil. Alliums are left out; lemon, ginger and roasted cumin carry the dressing.

- **Diet:** vegetarian, sattvic, no onion, no garlic, egg-free
- **Course:** Lunch
- **Cuisine:** Mediterranean
- **Prep:** 15 min
- **Cook:** 30 min
- **Yield:** 4 people
- **Allergens (estimate):** gluten, mustard

## Ingredients

- 1 small cauliflower, cut into florets
- 2 carrots, sliced on the diagonal
- 2 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
- 1 tsp organic ground cumin
- 1 tsp organic smoked paprika
- natural fine sea salt
- organic cracked black pepper
- 150 g couscous
- 200 ml boiling vegetable stock
- Juice of ½ lemon, plus extra to taste
- 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
- natural fine sea salt
- organic cracked black pepper
- tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- tsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tsp organic maple syrup
- natural fine sea salt
- organic cracked black pepper
- To assemble:
- 2 large handfuls lamb's lettuce

## Method

1. Start by getting the vegetables roasting.
2. Toss the cauliflower florets and sliced carrots with olive oil, cumin, smoked paprika, salt and pepper, then spread them out on a tray and roast until tender and caramelised at the edges.
3. While the vegetables are cooking, place the couscous in a large bowl and pour over the hot vegetable stock.
4. Cover and leave it to steam for a few minutes until fluffy, then loosen the grains gently with a fork.
5. Season with a pinch of salt and black pepper, then stir through the lemon juice and olive oil to keep it light and fragrant.
6. Taste and adjust with a little extra lemon if needed.
7. Whisk together all the dressing ingredients until smooth and well balanced — it should be bright, slightly sweet and gently sharp.
8. Once the vegetables are roasted, let them cool slightly before folding them through the couscous.
9. Toss everything together so the flavours mingle.
10. Just before serving, gently fold through the lamb’s lettuce so it stays fresh and vibrant.
11. Drizzle over the dressing and give everything one final toss.
12. Serve slightly warm or at room temperature with an extra squeeze of lemon over the top for a fresh lift.
13. It’s lovely on its own or alongside roasted chickpeas for something more substantial.
