Air Fryer Corn with Paprika and Peanuts

Main Course · Prep 5 min · Cook 15 min · 4 people

Air Fryer Corn with Paprika and Peanuts is a sattvic vegetarian dish of fresh corn cobs, organic extra virgin olive oil, organic paprika. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Ingredients

  • 4 fresh corn cobs
  • 2 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 tsp organic paprika
  • natural fine sea salt
  • organic cracked black pepper
  • 2 tbsp water
  • Lime wedges
  • Chopped coriander
  • Sliced red chillies
  • roasted peanuts (crushed)

Method

  1. Line the air fryer basket with parchment paper made for air fryers.
  2. Pour a small splash of water (about 2 tbsp) onto the parchment to create a touch of steam and keep the corn juicy.
  3. Brush the corn evenly with olive oil, making sure all the kernels get a good coating.
  4. Sprinkle over the paprika, then season generously with salt and pepper.
  5. Arrange the cobs in a single layer on top of the parchment.
  6. Air fry at 200°C for 12 minutes, turning once halfway through, until the corn is lightly charred and golden in spots.
  7. Serve hot straight from the fryer with a squeeze of lime, a scattering of coriander, fresh sliced red chillies, and a generous sprinkle of crushed roasted peanuts.

Almond Pesto With Roasted Tomato

Almond Pesto With Roasted Tomato is a sattvic vegetarian dish of organic extra virgin olive oil, cherry tomatoes, organic spaghetti. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Ingredients

  • ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
  • 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
  • 500 g cherry tomatoes
  • 400 g organic spaghetti
  • Natural fine sea salt
  • organic cracked black pepper
  • 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
  • 50 g organic almonds
  • 100 g vegetarian hard cheese or nutritional yeast or plant-based cheese
  • handful of basil
  • 1/2 lemon
  • natural fine sea salt

Method

  1. To make the pesto, place the almonds in a frying pan and roast over a medium to high heat until they start to colour.
  2. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
  3. This should take 3-4 minutes, then remove from the pan and allow to cool.
  4. Roughly chop the ginger and juice the lemon then place in a food processor with the almonds and the rest of the pesto ingredients.
  5. Blitz until roughly chopped but not completely smooth.
  6. Next, place the tomatoes in a baking tray, drizzle with the olive oil and season generously with salt and pepper.
  7. Roast in the oven for 20 minutes at 200c then set aside in a warm place.
  8. Cook the spaghetti according to packet instructions, drain (reserving 2 tbsp of the cooking water) then add back to the saucepan.
  9. Mix in the pesto along with the cooking water and serve with the roasted tomatoes on top.

Kitchen notes

Apple and Pecan Tart

Dessert · Prep 15 min · Cook 30 min · 4

Apple and Pecan Tart is an egg-free vegetarian sweet for a Vedanta kitchen — built around apples, butter or plant based alternative, organic brown sugar. Offer it fresh, without onion, garlic or eggs.

Ingredients

  • 2 apples
  • 50 g butter or plant based alternative
  • 1 tbsp organic brown sugar
  • 1 tbsp organic maple syrup
  • 1 tsp organic vanilla extract
  • 1 tsp organic plain flour
  • 1 large sheet of ready rolled puff pastry
  • 50 g organic pecans

Method

  1. Peel 2 apples, slice into quarters, removed the core then finely slice.
  2. Place the apples in a frying pan with the butter, sugar and maple syrup.
  3. Cook on a medium heat for 2-3 minutes, stirring gently so that the apples don’t break up.
  4. Add the vanilla extract and flour and cook for a further 2-3 minutes, again stirring gently.
  5. Set aside and let cool.
  6. Now place the pecans in a frying pan and gently toast until they become golden.
  7. Allow to cool, then roughly chop.
  8. Next, take the sheet of pastry and lay on a piece of baking paper.
  9. Place a plate roughly the same size as the pastry on top and cut around to create a circle.
  10. Take a sharp knife and lightly cut a smaller circle about 1cm from the edge. Don’t cut right through the pastry, just make an indent.
  11. Now neatly lay the cooled apples on top of the pastry.
  12. Bake at 200c for 20 minutes or until the pastry is golden brown.
  13. Scatter the chopped pecans on top and serve warm or allow to cool.

Kitchen notes

Plant-Based Minestrone is a sattvic vegetarian dish of organic extra virgin olive oil, carrots, celery sticks. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Ingredients

  • ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
  • 2 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil, finely chopped
  • 2 carrots, diced
  • 2 celery sticks, diced
  • 1 red pepper, diced
  • 1 tsp organic dried rosemary
  • 1 tsp organic dried thyme
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 2 tbsp tomato purée
  • 1 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 1.2 litres vegetable stock
  • 120 g small pasta
  • 2 handfuls cavolo nero, shredded
  • natural fine sea salt
  • organic cracked black pepper

Method

  1. Heat the olive oil in a large pot over a medium heat, then add the celery, carrot, and celery.
  2. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
  3. Season with salt and pepper, and cook gently for about 8 minutes until softened.
  4. Stir in the ginger, red pepper, rosemary, thyme and bay leaf.
  5. Cook for another 3–4 minutes before adding the tomato purée, cook for a further minute.
  6. Tip in the chopped tomatoes and vegetable stock, bring to a gentle boil, then lower to a simmer.
  7. Let the soup cook gently for 15 minutes before adding the pasta.
  8. Cook until al dente, about 10 minutes.
  9. If the soup thickens too much, loosen with a splash more stock or water.
  10. Stir through the cavolo nero just before serving so it wilts into the broth.
  11. Ladle into bowls, scatter with basil, a squeeze of lemon juice, a drizzle of oil and a generous grating of vegetarian hard cheese.
  12. Serve with plenty of crusty bread to mop up the broth.

Kitchen notes

  • Vedanta kitchen: vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no eggs, no mushrooms, no alcohol.
  • Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.
  • Hing is bloomed in hot ghee or oil; a pinch is enough.

Potato and Spinach Salad

Main Course · Prep 15 min · Cook 20 min · 4 People

Potato and Spinach Salad is a sattvic salad of new potatoes, organic chickpeas (cooked), celery sticks. Alliums are left out; lemon, ginger and roasted cumin carry the dressing.

Ingredients

  • 500 g new potatoes
  • 240 g organic chickpeas (cooked)
  • 3 celery sticks, finely sliced
  • 2 large handfuls baby spinach
  • 3 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • 1 tbsp mayonnaise, or plant-based alternative
  • natural fine sea salt
  • organic cracked black pepper
  • ½ tsp roasted cumin powder
  • ½ tsp fresh ginger, grated

Method

  1. Bring a pan of salted water to the boil and cook the new potatoes for around 15 to 20 minutes until just tender.
  2. Drain well and leave them to cool slightly then cut in half.
  3. In a small bowl, whisk together the olive oil, lemon juice and mayo until smooth.
  4. Season generously with salt and black pepper.
  5. In a large bowl, combine the warm potatoes with the chickpeas, celery, coriander stems and spinach.
  6. Pour over the dressing while the potatoes are still a little warm so they soak up the flavour.
  7. Toss well until everything is coated and serve.

Ingredients

  • 125 g organic butter
  • 130 g organic maple syrup
  • 125 g organic peanut butter
  • 350 g organic gluten free porridge oats
  • 2 tbsp organic pumpkin protein powder
  • 2 tbsp organic hulled hemp seed

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 180c/gas mark 4.
  2. Line an 8inch/20cm square tin with baking paper.
  3. Melt the butter in the saucepan over a low heat and stir in the maple syrup.
  4. Remove pan from the heat and stir in the peanut butter.

  6. Stir in the liquid mixture and combine.
  7. Pour the mixture into the baking tray and press down firmly.
  8. Bake for 25mins or until golden.
  9. Allow to cool and then cut into 12 squares.

Raspberry No-Bake Cheesecake

Raspberry No-Bake Cheesecake is an egg-free vegetarian sweet for a Vedanta kitchen — built around oat biscuits, plant-based butter, plant-based cream cheese. Offer it fresh, without onion, garlic or eggs.

Ingredients

  • 200 g oat biscuits, check they’re vegan
  • 80 g plant-based butter, melted
  • 400 g plant-based cream cheese
  • 120 g organic raw cane sugar
  • Juice of half a lemon
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 200 ml plant-based double cream
  • 200 g raspberries, fresh or frozen
  • 25 g soft light brown sugar
  • 1 oat biscuit, crushed
  • 8 rasberries

Method

  1. To make the base, crush the biscuits into fine crumbs, then stir through the melted butter.
  2. Divide between 8 glasses, press down gently with the back of a spoon, then pop into the fridge to set while you make the filling.
  3. For the filling, dissolve the brown sugar by warming it gently with a splash of water, then leave it to cool completely.
  4. Whip the cream until soft peaks form.
  5. In another bowl, beat the cream cheese with the cooled sugar syrup, lemon juice and vanilla until smooth, then fold in the whipped cream.
  6. To make the raspberry topping, put the raspberries and sugar into a small pan and heat gently until the fruit begins to break down.
  7. Remove from the heat, then leave to cool.
  8. Add a large spoonful of the filling followed by a large spoonful of the cooled raspberry sauce to each of the glasses with the biscuit base.
  9. Add a second spoonful of the filling, gently smooth down with a knife, then place in the fridge for at least an hour to set.
  10. Serve each glass with some of the crushed oat biscuit and a raspberry on top.

Roasted Aubergine and Cashew Soup

Roasted Aubergine and Cashew Soup is a sattvic vegetarian dish of organic extra virgin olive oil, aubergine, celery stalk. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Ingredients

  • ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
  • 1 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
  • 500 g aubergine, diced
  • 1 celery stalk
  • 1 tsp organic thyme
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 50 g sun-dried tomatoes, plus 30 g walnuts, chopped
  • 1 tbsp organic fresh lemon juice
  • 1 litre vegetable stock
  • natural fine sea salt
  • organic cracked black pepper

Method

  1. Roughly chop the aubergine and place in a large frying pan with 1 tbsp of olive oil.
  2. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.
  3. Fry on a medium to high heat for 10 minutes until any water evaporates and the aubergine colour.
  4. Remove the aubergine and set aside.
  5. Dice the celery and celery and add to the same pan with 1 tbsp of olive oil.
  6. Cook on a medium for 5 minutes stirring frequently.
  7. Soak the soaked sun-dried tomatoes in hot water for 5 minutes then drain and roughly chop.
  8. Chop the ginger and add to the pan along with soaked sun-dried tomatoes as well as the thyme and bay leaf, cook for a further 5 minutes.
  9. Add the lemon juice and cook for another 2 minutes before adding back the aubergine (reserve a few for garnish).
  10. Pour in the vegetable stock and bring to the boil then simmer for 30 minutes.
  11. Remove the bay leaf then liquidise with a stick blender.
  12. Serve with a couple of the reserved aubergine finely chopped.

Kitchen notes

  • Use stainless steel, cast iron, glass or clay — never aluminium.

Roasted Carrot and Coriander Soup

Roasted Carrot and Coriander Soup is a sattvic vegetarian dish of carrots, organic extra virgin olive oil, organic ground coriander. No onion or garlic — hing bloomed in hot oil gives the savoury depth. Cook fresh and serve as prasad-style kitchen food.

Ingredients

  • ¼ tsp hing (asafoetida)
  • 600 g carrots, peeled and cut into chunks
  • 2 tbsp organic extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 tsp organic ground coriander
  • 1 tsp organic ground cumin
  • 1 litre vegetable stock
  • 1 tbsp yoghurt or plant-based alternative
  • A large handful of fresh coriander
  • Natural Fine Sea Salt
  • Organic Cracked Black Pepper
  • A few extra coriander leaves
  • 2-3 organic walnuts (crushed), chopped
  • A few extra coriander leaves
  • 2-3 organic walnuts (crushed), chopped

Method

  1. Preheat the oven to 200°C.
  2. Place the carrots, celery and ginger cloves on a baking tray, drizzle with olive oil, season with salt and pepper, and roast for 25 minutes until golden and tender.
  3. Warm a little ghee or olive oil and bloom the hing for a few seconds until fragrant, then continue.

  7. Pour in the vegetable stock, bring to the boil and simmer for 10 minutes.
  8. Add the fresh coriander and yoghurt, then blend the soup until smooth using a stick blender or food processor. Adjust the seasoning to taste.
