Paneer tikka (dahi-ajwain, no garlic)

Signature starter from Punjab, cooked without onion or garlic.

Method

  1. Mise en place. Weigh 400 g paneer and 1 cup hung dahi. Set a cast-iron grill or steel oven tray — never aluminium foil. Confirm chilli powder and garam masala have no onion or garlic powder.
  2. Cut paneer into even 2.5 cm cubes. Pat very dry. Cut capsicum and tomato into similar squares so they cook in the same time.
  3. In a steel bowl whisk hung dahi with 1 tsp ajwain, 1 tsp Kashmiri chilli, turmeric, garam masala, salt and crushed ginger to a thick marinade that coats a spoon. No garlic paste.
  4. Fold paneer and vegetables through the marinade until every face is coated. Cover. Marinate 1 hour refrigerated (or 20 minutes at the pass if you are short of time).
  5. Thread onto steel skewers, alternating paneer and vegetable, leaving a little space between cubes so heat can circulate.
  6. Heat the cast-iron grill to medium-high until a drop of water dances. Brush with ghee. Lay skewers. Do not crowd.
  7. Grill 3–4 minutes per side until the dahi takes charred spots and the paneer is hot through. Turn with tongs. Brush once more with ghee.
  8. Doneness: edges blistered, centre hot, dahi set not wet. Rest 1 minute so juices settle.
  9. Finish with lemon juice at the pass. Serve 1 skewer per portion, hot. Hold no more than 10 minutes or the tikka steams and goes rubbery.

Chef notes

For Paneer tikka (dahi-ajwain, no garlic): keep the mix slightly drier than you think; wet mixes split or go greasy. Bloom hing in hot fat 20–30 seconds; raw hing tastes medicinal. Commercial hing is often cut with wheat flour — use a gluten-free hing if you must mark Gluten Free. Never cook tomato, tamarind, lemon, yogurt or milk in aluminium; use stainless steel, iron, clay or glass. Spice blends: read the packet. Many garam masalas hide onion or garlic powder. Pat paneer dry and do not boil it hard or it turns rubbery. Bring yogurt to a simmer gently and stir; a rolling boil can split it.

Service notes

Serve immediately while crisp or hot. Offer a chutney or raita that is also onion-garlic free. Service temperature: Hot.
