## Method

1. Mise en place. Weigh 2 cups atta, 1 cup ghee, 1 cup powdered sugar or jaggery, almonds and cardamom. Set a heavy steel kadhai — never aluminium.
2. Melt ghee. Add atta. Roast on low 18–22 minutes, stirring constantly, until the atta is biscuit-brown and smells like toasted wheat. Optional gond: fry in ghee first until it puffs, crush, add at the end.
3. Cool 8–10 minutes — sugar will melt if the mix is scorching hot. Stir in sugar, crushed cardamom and chopped almonds.
4. While still warm, press into 40–45 g pinnis. Pack firmly so they do not crumble.
5. Doneness: roasted aroma, no raw atta, holds shape when cool.
6. Cool fully. Store in a steel tin. Serve at room temperature. Label gluten, milk (ghee) and tree nuts.

## Chef notes

For Pinni (atta-ghee-nuts): keep the mix slightly drier than you think; wet mixes split or go greasy. Bloom hing in hot fat 20–30 seconds; raw hing tastes medicinal. Commercial hing is often cut with wheat flour — use a gluten-free hing if you must mark Gluten Free. Never cook tomato, tamarind, lemon, yogurt or milk in aluminium; use stainless steel, iron, clay or glass. Spice blends: read the packet. Many garam masalas hide onion or garlic powder.
