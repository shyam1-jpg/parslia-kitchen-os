## Method

1. Mise en place. Soak 1/2 cup rice 30 minutes. Grind to a wet paste. Measure 1 L milk, sugar, saffron, cardamom. Heavy stainless steel milk pot and clay bowls — never aluminium.
2. Bring milk to a simmer. Stir in rice paste. Cook on low 20–25 minutes, stirring, until the phirni is thick and the rice is cooked, no grit.
3. Add sugar, saffron soaked in 1 tbsp warm milk, and cardamom. Cook 5 minutes more. It thickens further as it cools.
4. Pour into clay bowls. Cool. Chill at least 2 hours.
5. Doneness: sets to a soft quiver, grains fine, saffron aroma. No skin if you press cling on the surface.
6. Serve chilled. Hold refrigerated up to 24 hours. Label milk.

## Chef notes

For Phirni (Punjabi rice pudding): keep the mix slightly drier than you think; wet mixes split or go greasy. Bloom hing in hot fat 20–30 seconds; raw hing tastes medicinal. Commercial hing is often cut with wheat flour — use a gluten-free hing if you must mark Gluten Free. Never cook tomato, tamarind, lemon, yogurt or milk in aluminium; use stainless steel, iron, clay or glass. Spice blends: read the packet. Many garam masalas hide onion or garlic powder.
