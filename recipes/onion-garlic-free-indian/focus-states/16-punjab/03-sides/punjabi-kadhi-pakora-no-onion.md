|-----|------|--------|------------|
| 200 | g | — | plain yogurt |
| 150 | g | — | besan (gram flour) |
| ½ | tsp | ≈ 1.2 g | turmeric powder |
| 1 | tsp | ≈ 6 g | salt, or to taste |
| 1 | tbsp | ≈ 14 g | ghee, plus cumin, methi, dry chilli, pinch hing |

## Method

1. Mise en place. Whisk yogurt and besan for the kadhi until lump-free. Mix a thicker besan batter for pakora with ajwain, chilli and optional spinach. Set a steel kadhai for frying and a heavy pot for simmering — never aluminium.
2. Heat frying oil to 175 C. Drop spoonfuls of pakora batter. Fry 4–5 minutes until gold and cooked through. Drain on a steel rack.
3. Thin the yogurt-besan mix with water. Pour into the pot. Add turmeric and salt. Bring to a gentle simmer, stirring so it does not split. Cook 20–25 minutes until the raw besan smell is gone and the kadhi coats a spoon.
4. Slip in pakora. Simmer 5 minutes so they soak but still hold a centre.
5. Tadka: ghee, cumin, methi seeds, dry chilli, hing — 20–30 seconds. Pour over. Cover 1 minute.
6. Doneness: kadhi silky, not chalky; pakora soft-centred. If it splits, whisk off the heat and do not boil hard.
7. Serve hot with rice. Hold on the lowest heat, stirred. Pakora continue to swell — add a splash of hot water if it thickens too far.

## Chef notes

For Punjabi kadhi pakora (no onion): keep the mix slightly drier than you think; wet mixes split or go greasy. Bloom hing in hot fat 20–30 seconds; raw hing tastes medicinal. Commercial hing is often cut with wheat flour — use a gluten-free hing if you must mark Gluten Free. Never cook tomato, tamarind, lemon, yogurt or milk in aluminium; use stainless steel, iron, clay or glass. Spice blends: read the packet. Many garam masalas hide onion or garlic powder. Bring yogurt to a simmer gently and stir; a rolling boil can split it. Cook or roast besan until the raw smell is gone.

## Service notes

Serve family-style in a steel bowl. Keep a lid on at the pass so it does not skin. Service temperature: Hot.
