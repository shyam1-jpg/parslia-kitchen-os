## Method

1. Mise en place. Wipe 3 large brinjals. Set a cast-iron grill or open flame and a steel kadhai — never aluminium.
2. Prick brinjals. Roast, turning, until the skin is charred and the flesh collapses, 15–20 minutes. Cool slightly. Peel. Mash with a fork — leave some texture.
3. Heat ghee in the kadhai. Bloom cumin and hing 20–30 seconds. Add ginger and green chilli. Add chopped tomato. Cook 6–8 minutes until jammy.
4. Add mashed baingan, salt and coriander. Cook 8–10 minutes, stirring, until the bharta looks dry-ish and smells smoky, not raw.
5. Doneness: no watery pool, smoky aroma, tomato melted. Taste salt and chilli.
6. Serve hot with roti. Hold covered. The dish improves after 10 minutes rest. Label nightshade if needed.

## Chef notes

For Baingan bharta (no onion garlic): keep the mix slightly drier than you think; wet mixes split or go greasy. Bloom hing in hot fat 20–30 seconds; raw hing tastes medicinal. Commercial hing is often cut with wheat flour — use a gluten-free hing if you must mark Gluten Free. Never cook tomato, tamarind, lemon, yogurt or milk in aluminium; use stainless steel, iron, clay or glass. Spice blends: read the packet. Many garam masalas hide onion or garlic powder.

## Service notes

Serve family-style in a steel bowl. Keep a lid on at the pass so it does not skin. Service temperature: Hot.
