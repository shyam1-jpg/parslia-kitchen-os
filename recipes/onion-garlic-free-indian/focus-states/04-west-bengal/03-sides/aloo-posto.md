## Method

1. Mise en place. Weigh every ingredient on this card for 4 portions. Set Stainless steel kadhai or saucepan — never aluminium. Wash produce. Confirm spice blends have no onion or garlic powder. No onion, no garlic, no aluminium.
2. Cut vegetables to even size so they cook together. If using potato, cook until tender, drain, and steam-dry so the mix is not wet.
3. Cook potatoes, add posto. Work in Stainless steel kadhai or saucepan — never aluminium. Cook to the doneness below, tasting salt at the end. Do not add onion, garlic or aluminium cookware.
4. Oil should shine. Work in Stainless steel kadhai or saucepan — never aluminium. Cook to the doneness below, tasting salt at the end. Do not add onion, garlic or aluminium cookware.
5. No peyaj, no rasun. Work in Stainless steel kadhai or saucepan — never aluminium. Cook to the doneness below, tasting salt at the end. Do not add onion, garlic or aluminium cookware.
6. Doneness: vegetables yield to a knife, gravy coats a spoon, salt is balanced. Rest 2 minutes off the heat so seasoning settles.
7. Taste and adjust salt, lemon or chilli only — do not add onion or garlic at the finish. Garnish as listed. Yield is 4 portions.
8. Hold hot above 63 C if the pass is delayed, or cool quickly and reheat once only to piping hot. Do not hold in aluminium. Service: Hot.

## Chef notes

For Aloo Posto: keep the mix slightly drier than you think; wet mixes split or go greasy. Bloom hing in hot fat 20–30 seconds; raw hing tastes medicinal. Commercial hing is often cut with wheat flour — use a gluten-free hing if you must mark Gluten Free. Never cook tomato, tamarind, lemon, yogurt or milk in aluminium; use stainless steel, iron, clay or glass. Spice blends: read the packet. Many garam masalas hide onion or garlic powder.

## Service notes

Serve family-style in a steel bowl. Keep a lid on at the pass so it does not skin. Service temperature: Hot.
