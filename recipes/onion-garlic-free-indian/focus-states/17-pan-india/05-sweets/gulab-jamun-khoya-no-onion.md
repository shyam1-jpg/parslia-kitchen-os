## Method

1. Mise en place. Mix khoya and a little maida to a smooth dough — no cracks, no extra flour. Sugar syrup with rose and cardamom, one-string, warm. Steel kadhai of ghee — never aluminium.
2. Shape 16 smooth balls. If they crack, knead with a drop of milk.
3. Fry on low-medium, 6–8 minutes, turning, until deep brown. High heat colours the outside and leaves a raw centre.
4. Drain 30 seconds. Soak in warm syrup 30 minutes, turning once.
5. Doneness: balls swollen, syrup in the centre, no doughy core. Serve warm, 2 per portion.
6. Hold in syrup. Label milk and gluten.

## Chef notes

For Gulab jamun (khoya, no onion): keep the mix slightly drier than you think; wet mixes split or go greasy. Bloom hing in hot fat 20–30 seconds; raw hing tastes medicinal. Commercial hing is often cut with wheat flour — use a gluten-free hing if you must mark Gluten Free. Never cook tomato, tamarind, lemon, yogurt or milk in aluminium; use stainless steel, iron, clay or glass. Spice blends: read the packet. Many garam masalas hide onion or garlic powder.
