Athens Travel Guide

Athens is where Western democracy, philosophy and theatre took shape, and the Acropolis still towers over the city as a reminder. But modern Athens is no open-air museum. It is a sprawling, energetic, sometimes gritty capital with excellent food, a thriving street-art scene, rooftop bars facing the Parthenon and neighbourhoods that come alive after dark. Many travellers treat it as a quick stop before the islands; those who give it two or three days usually wish they had stayed longer.

When to Go

April to June and September to October offer warm, sunny weather that is comfortable for climbing hills and exploring ruins. July and August are very hot, and heat waves can lead to midday closures at archaeological sites; plan visits for early morning. Many Athenians head to the islands in August, so the city feels quieter. November to March is mild and rainier, with short queues at the Acropolis and lower hotel rates, though some island ferries reduce their schedules.

Top Attractions

  1. The Acropolis – The Parthenon, the Erechtheion with its Caryatids, and the Propylaea gateway. Book timed tickets and arrive at opening time to avoid heat and crowds.
  2. Acropolis Museum – A modern museum at the foot of the hill, with a glass floor over excavations and a top floor that displays the Parthenon sculptures in their original arrangement.
  3. Plaka – The old neighbourhood below the Acropolis, full of tavernas, souvenir shops and neoclassical houses; continue up into tiny Anafiotika, which feels like an island village.
  4. Ancient Agora and Temple of Hephaestus – The marketplace where Socrates taught, with one of the best-preserved Greek temples.
  5. Syntagma Square and the Changing of the Guard – The Evzones guards change outside the Parliament every hour, with a larger ceremony on Sunday mornings.
  6. National Archaeological Museum – One of the world's great collections of ancient Greek art.
  7. Lycabettus Hill – Hike or take the funicular for sunset views over the whole city to the sea.
  8. Cape Sounion – A half-day coastal trip to the Temple of Poseidon, famous for its sunset.

Food to Try

Greek food in Athens ranges from cheap street snacks to inventive modern tavernas. Try souvlaki and gyros wrapped in pita, spanakopita (spinach pie), moussaka, horiatiki (Greek salad), fava (yellow split-pea purée) and gemista (stuffed tomatoes and peppers). For dessert, sample loukoumades (honey doughnuts) and Greek yoghurt with honey. Vegetarians have many options because of the Orthodox fasting tradition. The Central Market (Varvakios Agora) and neighbourhoods like Koukaki, Psyrri and Pangrati have excellent tavernas.

Getting There and Getting Around

Athens International Airport (ATH), Eleftherios Venizelos, is linked to the centre by Metro Line 3, the suburban railway and express buses. Piraeus, the main port for island ferries, is connected by Metro Line 1 and tram; Rafina and Lavrio serve some Cyclades routes.

The centre is compact and walkable, and the metro is clean and efficient, with ancient finds displayed in stations such as Syntagma and Acropolis. Trams run to the coast. Taxis are yellow and reasonably priced; apps help avoid disputes.

Where to Stay

  • Plaka and Monastiraki: Right below the Acropolis, perfect for first-timers but touristy.
  • Koukaki and Makrygianni: Quiet residential streets near the Acropolis Museum, with good cafés.
  • Syntagma: Central and well connected, with many business hotels.
  • Psyrri: Lively nightlife and street art.
  • Kolonaki: Upscale shopping and dining near Lycabettus.
  • Athens Riviera (Glyfada, Vouliagmeni): Beaches and resorts on the coast for a city-and-sea combination.

Budget Tips

  • A combined ticket covers the Acropolis and several other major archaeological sites; check whether it suits your plans.
  • Some sites have free admission days at certain times of year; check the official Greek culture ministry listings.
  • Eat souvlaki and bakery snacks for cheap, filling meals.
  • Drink tap water, which is safe in Athens.
  • Book island ferries in advance for summer, and compare fast and conventional boats.
  • Hotels are typically cheaper in winter and midweek.
