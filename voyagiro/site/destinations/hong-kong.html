Hong Kong Travel Guide

Overview

Hong Kong is one of the world's most dramatic cityscapes: a forest of skyscrapers squeezed between mountains and a deep harbour, with green country parks and outlying islands only a short ride away. It blends Cantonese tradition with a long history as a global trading port, so you can visit a smoky incense-filled temple in the morning, hike a ridge trail at lunchtime and eat dim sum, roast goose and Michelin-starred food by evening. Transport is superb, distances are short, and it is an easy add-on to trips in mainland China, Macau or Southeast Asia.

Best Time to Visit

  • October to December: Usually the most pleasant months, with clear skies, lower humidity and comfortable hiking weather.
  • January to March: Cool and sometimes damp; Chinese New Year brings fireworks, flower markets and decorations, but some small businesses close for a few days and hotel prices rise.
  • April to May: Warming up, with increasing humidity and festivals such as the Cheung Chau Bun Festival.
  • June to September: Hot, very humid and rainy, with the risk of typhoons. When a typhoon signal is raised, ferries, outdoor attractions and offices may close, so build flexibility into your plans.

Top Attractions

  1. Victoria Peak – Ride the historic Peak Tram or a bus to the top for the classic skyline view; walk the Peak Circle trail for quieter viewpoints.
  2. Star Ferry – A short, inexpensive harbour crossing between Tsim Sha Tsui and Central that ranks among the world's great ferry rides.
  3. Tsim Sha Tsui Promenade – Skyline views across the harbour and the nightly Symphony of Lights show.
  4. Temple Street Night Market – Street food, fortune tellers and stalls in Yau Ma Tei.
  5. Tian Tan Buddha and Ngong Ping – A giant bronze Buddha on Lantau Island, reached by the Ngong Ping 360 cable car, near Po Lin Monastery.
  6. Man Mo Temple and Hollywood Road – An atmospheric incense-filled temple surrounded by antique shops and galleries.
  7. Dragon's Back trail – A popular ridge hike on Hong Kong Island ending at Shek O or Big Wave Bay beach.
  8. M+ and West Kowloon – A major museum of visual culture on the harbourfront, plus the Hong Kong Palace Museum.

Food to Try

Cantonese cuisine shines here. Start with dim sum: har gow (shrimp dumplings), siu mai, char siu bao and cheung fun. Try roast goose and char siu (barbecue pork), wonton noodles, clay-pot rice in winter and street snacks like egg waffles and curry fish balls. Hong Kong-style milk tea and pineapple buns are best at a cha chaan teng (local café). Vegetarians can find Buddhist vegetarian restaurants, often near temples, and Chungking Mansions in Tsim Sha Tsui is known for affordable Indian and South Asian food.

Getting There and Around

Hong Kong International Airport (HKG) on Lantau Island connects to Central in under half an hour on the Airport Express. High-speed trains run from West Kowloon to mainland Chinese cities, and ferries and the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge link to Macau.

The MTR metro is fast, clean and covers most areas. Trams ("ding dings") on Hong Kong Island, buses, minibuses and ferries fill in the rest. Use an Octopus card (also available on phones) for transport and many shops. Taxis are colour-coded by area and relatively affordable.

Where to Stay

  • Central and Admiralty: Business district with luxury hotels and nightlife at Lan Kwai Fong and SoHo.
  • Wan Chai and Causeway Bay: Shopping, dining and many mid-range hotels.
  • Tsim Sha Tsui: Harbour views, museums and a huge range of hotels from guesthouses to five stars.
  • Mong Kok and Yau Ma Tei: Busy local streets and markets, generally better value.
  • Sheung Wan: Characterful old district with boutique stays.
  • Lantau and outlying islands: Quieter, near the airport and nature.

Budget Tips

  • Eat at cha chaan tengs, dai pai dongs (open-air stalls) and noodle shops.
  • Ride the Star Ferry and trams, two of the cheapest scenic experiences anywhere.
  • Hiking trails, beaches, the harbourfront and many temples are free.
  • Some museums have free admission days; check before you go.
  • Hotel rooms are small and pricey; compare locations along MTR lines.

Practical Information

  • Visa: Entry rules change and depend on your passport. Use the destination country's official government site and your own government's travel advice. This guide does not say who can enter.
  • Currency: Hong Kong dollar (HKD). Octopus, cards and mobile wallets are widely accepted.
  • Language: Cantonese and English are official; Mandarin is widely spoken.
  • Safety: This guide does not rate safety. Read your government's current travel advice before you go.
