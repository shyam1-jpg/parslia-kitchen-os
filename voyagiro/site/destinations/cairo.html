Cairo Travel Guide

Cairo is overwhelming in the best sense. Africa's largest city sprawls along the Nile, its skyline a mix of medieval minarets and modern towers, its streets loud with traffic and commerce. On the edge of town, the Pyramids of Giza have stood for more than four thousand years. Few places pack so much history into a single trip, and with a little planning, Cairo is far easier to enjoy than its reputation suggests.

Essentials

  • Currency: Egyptian pound (EGP)
  • Language: Arabic; English is common in tourist areas
  • Airport: Cairo International (CAI); Sphinx International serves some routes near Giza
  • Dress: Modest clothing is respectful, especially at mosques

The Best Months

October to April is the most comfortable time, with warm, sunny days and cooler nights; December and January can feel chilly after dark. This is high season, so book key tours ahead.

May to September is very hot, often uncomfortably so in the middle of the day. Sightseeing is still possible if you start early and rest in the afternoon, and hotel rates are usually lower.

During Ramadan, the city's rhythm changes: daytime is quieter, some places have shorter hours, and evenings are festive after sunset. Check the dates when planning.

Sights You Should Not Miss

The Pyramids of Giza and the Sphinx

The Great Pyramid, the pyramids of Khafre and Menkaure, and the Great Sphinx form one of the most famous sights on earth. Go at opening time for cooler temperatures and fewer crowds, and consider a licensed guide.

The Grand Egyptian Museum

Near the pyramids, this vast museum is designed to house the treasures of Tutankhamun and a huge collection of antiquities. Check which galleries are open before you visit.

The Egyptian Museum, Tahrir

The historic museum in central Cairo still holds a remarkable collection in a classic old building.

Islamic Cairo

Explore the Citadel of Saladin and the Mosque of Muhammad Ali, then wander historic Al-Muizz Street, lined with mosques and madrasas. The Khan el-Khalili bazaar is the place for spices, lamps and souvenirs.

Coptic Cairo

The Hanging Church, the Coptic Museum and the Ben Ezra Synagogue reveal the city's Christian and Jewish heritage.

Saqqara and Dahshur

South of the city, the Step Pyramid of Djoser and the Bent and Red Pyramids are often quieter than Giza.

The Nile

Take a felucca sailboat ride at sunset or a dinner cruise.

Food

Egyptian cuisine is friendly to vegetarians. Koshari, a mix of rice, lentils, pasta, chickpeas, tomato sauce and fried onions, is the national comfort food. Ful medames (stewed fava beans) and ta'ameya (Egyptian falafel made with fava beans) are breakfast staples. Try molokhia soup, mahshi (stuffed vegetables), grilled kofta and sweet basbousa or om ali. Drink bottled water.

Getting Around

Cairo International Airport is east of the city. Pre-booked transfers or ride-hailing apps avoid the haggling of street taxis. The Cairo Metro is cheap and useful for the centre and Coptic Cairo, with women-only carriages. Ride-hailing apps are the simplest way to reach Giza and the Citadel. Traffic is heavy, so allow extra time. Trains and domestic flights connect Cairo with Luxor and Aswan.

Where to Stay

  • Giza: Hotels with pyramid views; good for early starts at the plateau.
  • Downtown and Zamalek: Central, with Zamalek an island district of leafy streets, galleries and cafés.
  • Garden City and the Corniche: Large international hotels along the Nile.
  • Heliopolis and New Cairo: Closer to the airport.

Tips for Your Budget

  • A combination of the metro and ride-hailing is much cheaper than private drivers.
  • Eat at local koshari and ta'ameya shops.
  • Agree on prices before any ride, camel trip or service, and politely decline unsolicited guides.
  • Carry small notes for tips, known as baksheesh, which are part of daily life.
  • Visit in shoulder months such as March, April, October and November.
