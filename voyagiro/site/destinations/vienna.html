Vienna Travel Guide

Vienna wears its imperial past with elegance. For centuries the Habsburgs ruled a vast empire from here, and they left behind grand palaces, the Ringstrasse boulevard lined with monumental buildings, and some of the world's richest art collections. The city also shaped Western music: Mozart, Beethoven, Haydn, Schubert and the Strauss family all worked here, and opera and classical concerts remain part of everyday cultural life. Add the famous coffee houses, a thriving wine scene within the city limits and excellent public transport, and it is easy to see why Vienna regularly ranks among the most liveable cities in the world.

When to Visit

Spring (April–May) brings blossoming parks and the start of outdoor café season. Summer (June–August) is warm, with open-air film festivals in front of the City Hall, the Danube Island Festival and swimming in the Old Danube; note that the State Opera and Vienna Boys' Choir take summer breaks. Autumn (September–October) is harvest time in the vineyards, with new wine served in the Heurigen taverns. Winter (November–February) is cold but magical, with Christmas markets from mid-November, the ball season in January and February, and cosy coffee-house afternoons.

What to See

  • Schönbrunn Palace – The Habsburg summer residence with ornate state rooms, vast formal gardens, the Gloriette viewpoint and the world's oldest zoo.
  • Hofburg – The former imperial palace in the city centre, home to the Imperial Apartments, the Sisi Museum and the Spanish Riding School.
  • St Stephen's Cathedral – The Gothic heart of Vienna with its patterned tile roof; climb the south tower for views.
  • Belvedere – Baroque palaces housing Austrian art, including Gustav Klimt's The Kiss.
  • Kunsthistorisches Museum – A grand fine-arts museum with works by Bruegel, Vermeer and Raphael.
  • MuseumsQuartier – A cultural complex with the Leopold Museum (Egon Schiele) and modern art spaces.
  • Vienna State Opera – See a performance, or buy inexpensive standing-room tickets on the day.
  • Naschmarkt and the Prater – A long open-air food market, and the amusement park with the historic Giant Ferris Wheel.

Food and Coffee

Start with a Wiener Schnitzel, a thin breaded veal cutlet, often served with potato salad. Try Tafelspitz (boiled beef with horseradish and apple sauce), goulash, and for vegetarians Käsespätzle (cheese noodles) and Kaiserschmarrn, a fluffy shredded pancake with plum compote. Coffee-house culture is listed as intangible cultural heritage: order a Melange and a slice of Sachertorte or apple strudel, and linger as long as you like. At a Würstelstand sausage stall, locals grab a late-night snack. The Naschmarkt has many international options, including Indian and Middle Eastern food.

Getting There and Getting Around

Vienna International Airport (VIE) is linked to the centre by the City Airport Train (CAT), the cheaper S-Bahn and buses. Vienna Hauptbahnhof is a major rail hub, with trains to Prague, Budapest, Salzburg, Munich and night trains across Europe.

Vienna's U-Bahn, trams and buses are efficient, clean and run late, with 24-hour metro service on weekend nights. Day and multi-day passes are good value. The historic centre inside the Ring is compact and walkable, and the Ring tram routes provide a ready-made sightseeing loop. City bikes are available for short trips.

Where to Stay

  • Innere Stadt (1st district): Inside the Ring, close to all major sights; the most expensive area.
  • Leopoldstadt (2nd): Near the Prater and the Danube canal, with good-value hotels.
  • Mariahilf and Neubau (6th–7th): Shopping streets, cafés and the MuseumsQuartier; trendy and central.
  • Wieden (4th): Close to the Naschmarkt and Belvedere, quieter streets.
  • Near the Hauptbahnhof (10th): Modern, practical for rail travellers and often cheaper.

Budget Tips

  • Buy standing-room tickets at the State Opera for a low price on performance days.
  • Use a transport pass; the Vienna City Card adds discounts on attractions.
  • Lunch menus (Mittagsmenü) in local restaurants are good value.
  • Tap water comes from Alpine springs and is excellent; drinking fountains are common in summer.
  • Many parks, the Schönbrunn gardens and the outdoor areas of the Belvedere are free.

Practical Information

  • Visa: Entry rules change and depend on your passport. Use the destination country's official government site and your own government's travel advice. This guide does not say who can enter.
  • Currency: Euro (EUR). Some small cafés and market stalls prefer cash.
  • Language: German (with a distinct Viennese dialect); English is widely understood.
  • Safety: This guide does not rate safety. Read your government's current travel advice before you go.
  • Shops: Most shops close on Sundays, though supermarkets in some stations open.
