Bangkok Travel Guide

First Impressions

Bangkok can feel overwhelming in the first hour and addictive by the third day. Gilded temple roofs rise above traffic-choked roads, longtail boats roar along the Chao Phraya, and street vendors cook remarkable food on a single wok at the kerb. The Thai capital is a city of contrasts: serene monasteries and buzzing night markets, gleaming malls and old canal-side communities. It is one of the most affordable big cities in Asia for visitors, and a natural gateway to Thailand's islands and the north.

Weather and When to Visit

Bangkok is hot all year, so think in terms of three seasons rather than four.

  • Cool season (November–February): The most comfortable period, with lower humidity and mostly dry days. This is peak tourist season, so hotels fill up around Christmas and New Year.
  • Hot season (March–May): Intensely hot. April brings Songkran, the Thai New Year, celebrated with city-wide water fights, which is a fun but very busy time.
  • Rainy season (June–October): Expect heavy afternoon downpours and occasional street flooding, but rain rarely lasts all day. Hotel prices are often lower and the city is greener.

Must-See Sights

  1. The Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew – The former royal residence and the Temple of the Emerald Buddha. A strict dress code applies: shoulders and knees covered.
  2. Wat Pho – Home of the giant Reclining Buddha and a renowned traditional Thai massage school.
  3. Wat Arun – The Temple of Dawn on the river's west bank, with steep, porcelain-studded towers; beautiful at sunset from across the river.
  4. Chao Phraya River – Ride the Chao Phraya Express Boat between piers for a cheap, scenic way to reach temples and markets.
  5. Chatuchak Weekend Market – A sprawling market with thousands of stalls selling everything from clothes to plants and street food.
  6. Chinatown (Yaowarat) – Best at night, when the main road turns into a street-food corridor.
  7. Jim Thompson House – A museum of traditional teak houses and Southeast Asian art.
  8. Lumphini Park – A green escape in the city centre, where you might spot monitor lizards by the lakes.

Food Culture

Eating is the heart of Bangkok life. Try pad thai, som tam (green papaya salad), tom yum goong, green curry, khao man gai (Hainanese chicken rice), boat noodles and mango sticky rice. Street food is generally safe at busy stalls with a high turnover. Vegetarians can look for the yellow "jay" flags, which mark vegan Chinese-Thai food, and Indian restaurants are concentrated around Pahurat ("Little India") and Sukhumvit. For a sit-down treat, Bangkok also has a celebrated fine-dining scene.

Getting In and Getting Around

Bangkok has two airports: Suvarnabhumi (BKK) for most international flights and Don Mueang (DMK) for many low-cost carriers. The Airport Rail Link runs from Suvarnabhumi into the city, and Don Mueang is connected by the SRT Red Line and buses. Always use the official taxi queue or a ride-hailing app.

Inside the city, the BTS Skytrain and MRT subway avoid the traffic and cover the main districts. River boats are perfect for the old town. Taxis should use the meter, and apps like Grab and Bolt help avoid haggling. Tuk-tuks are fun for short trips, but agree on a price first and decline offers of cheap "tours" that end at gem shops.

Areas to Stay

  • Sukhumvit: International restaurants, nightlife and good BTS access. A practical base with options for every budget.
  • Siam and Ratchaprasong: The shopping heart, central for both BTS lines.
  • Riverside: Grand hotels with views and boat access to the old town.
  • Rattanakosin and Banglamphu (incl. Khao San Road): Near the major temples, with budget guesthouses and a backpacker scene.
  • Silom and Sathorn: Business district by day, lively at night, close to Lumphini Park.

Money-Saving Ideas

  • Eat street food and at food courts in malls for excellent, inexpensive meals.
  • Use the river boats and public transport instead of taxis during rush hour.
  • Visit major temples early in the morning to avoid heat and tour groups.
  • Bargain politely at markets, but not in malls or 7-Elevens.
  • Travel during the rainy season for lower hotel rates.

Practical Notes

  • Visa: Entry rules change and depend on your passport. Use the destination country's official government site and your own government's travel advice. This guide does not say who can enter.
  • Currency: Thai baht (THB). Cash is useful for street food; ATMs charge a fee for foreign cards.
  • Language: Thai. English is common in tourist areas.
  • Safety and respect: Bangkok is generally safe. Watch out for scams near the Grand Palace ("it's closed today"), dress respectfully at temples, never disrespect the monarchy, and remove shoes when entering homes and temple halls.
