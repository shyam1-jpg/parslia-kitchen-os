Mumbai Travel Guide

The City That Never Sleeps

Mumbai is India's financial capital, home of Bollywood and a city of relentless energy. Built on seven islands joined by land reclamation, it stretches along the Arabian Sea from the colonial grandeur of South Mumbai to the beaches and creative suburbs of Bandra and Juhu. Gothic railway stations stand beside Art Deco apartment blocks, fishing villages sit in the shadow of skyscrapers, and street-food stalls feed millions every day. The pace can be intense, but evenings on Marine Drive, with the sea breeze and the "Queen's Necklace" of streetlights, show the city at its most relaxed.

When to Visit

  • November to February: The most comfortable season, warm and relatively dry, with pleasant evenings. Peak time for visitors and weddings.
  • March to May: Hot and humid before the monsoon.
  • June to September: The monsoon brings heavy, sometimes torrential rain that can flood roads and disrupt trains. It is dramatic and green, and hotel rates are usually lower, but plan for delays.
  • Festivals: Ganesh Chaturthi (usually August or September) is Mumbai's biggest festival, with huge processions and immersions at the sea. Diwali in October or November is also spectacular.

Top Attractions

  1. Gateway of India – The basalt arch on the Apollo Bunder waterfront, facing the historic Taj Mahal Palace hotel.
  2. Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus – A UNESCO-listed Victorian Gothic railway station, still one of the busiest in the country.
  3. Marine Drive – The seafront promenade, best at sunset, ending at Girgaon Chowpatty beach.
  4. Elephanta Caves – Rock-cut temples dedicated to Shiva on an island reached by ferry from the Gateway of India; closed on Mondays.
  5. Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya – The city's main museum, with Indian sculpture, miniature paintings and decorative arts, in Kala Ghoda.
  6. Dhobi Ghat – The vast open-air laundry near Mahalaxmi station, best seen from the bridge.
  7. Bandra – Bandstand promenade, the Bandra-Worli Sea Link views, Mount Mary Church and street art in Chapel Road.
  8. Siddhivinayak Temple and Haji Ali Dargah – Two of the city's most visited religious sites; Haji Ali is reached by a causeway at low tide.

Food to Try

Mumbai's street food is legendary. Try vada pav (spiced potato fritter in a bun), pav bhaji, bhel puri and sev puri on Chowpatty or Juhu beach, misal pav and pani puri. Explore the Irani cafés for bun maska, chai and berry pulao, and coastal Malvani and Konkani restaurants for fish thali, bombil fry and sol kadhi. Mohammed Ali Road is famous for kebabs and sweets, particularly during Ramadan nights. Vegetarians are spoilt for choice with Gujarati and Udupi restaurants. Drink bottled or filtered water, and choose busy stalls with freshly prepared food.

Getting There and Getting Around

Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport (BOM) handles domestic and international flights from two terminals; Navi Mumbai International Airport has added further capacity. Long-distance trains arrive at CSMT, Mumbai Central, Dadar and Bandra Terminus.

The suburban railway is the city's lifeline and fast, but extremely crowded at rush hour; first-class and women-only compartments are available, and AC locals run on some routes. The Metro lines are expanding and are a comfortable option where available. Black-and-yellow taxis and auto-rickshaws (in the suburbs) run on meters, and app cabs are widely used. The Coastal Road and Sea Link speed up north–south car journeys.

Where to Stay

  • Colaba and Fort: Heritage atmosphere, near the Gateway of India, museums and cafés.
  • Marine Drive and Churchgate: Sea views and Art Deco buildings.
  • Bandra: Trendy restaurants, nightlife and boutique stays.
  • Juhu: Beachfront hotels near the airport.
  • Andheri and the airport area: Practical for flights and business, with many mid-range hotels.
  • Lower Parel and BKC: Modern business hotels and malls.

Budget Tips

  • Use local trains outside rush hour and the Metro for cheap, fast travel.
  • Street food and Irani cafés offer excellent value.
  • Many experiences are free: Marine Drive, the beaches, Kala Ghoda's architecture and temples.
  • Compare hotels in Andheri or Bandra if South Mumbai is too expensive.
  • Avoid travelling during heavy monsoon warnings.

Practical Information

  • Visa: Entry rules change and depend on your passport. Use the destination country's official government site and your own government's travel advice. This guide does not say who can enter.
  • Currency: Indian rupee (INR). UPI and cards are widely accepted; carry some cash for small vendors.
  • Language: Marathi is the state language; Hindi and English are widely spoken.
  • Safety: This guide does not rate safety. Read your government's current travel advice before you go.
