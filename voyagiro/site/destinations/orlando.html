Orlando Travel Guide

Orlando, in central Florida, is the theme-park capital of the world. Walt Disney World, Universal Orlando Resort and SeaWorld draw families, thrill-seekers and fans from every continent. But the area also has natural springs, lakes, a lively food scene and the Kennedy Space Center within driving distance. Planning matters here more than almost anywhere: the parks are vast, ticket options are complex and the summer heat is intense.

Planning Checklist

  • Decide your parks first. Disney World alone has four theme parks (Magic Kingdom, EPCOT, Hollywood Studios and Animal Kingdom) and two water parks. Universal has Universal Studios Florida, Islands of Adventure, the newer Epic Universe and the Volcano Bay water park. Few visitors manage everything in one trip.
  • Buy tickets from official sources or authorised sellers, and compare multi-day options, which usually cost less per day.
  • Check paid queue-skipping systems, such as Disney's Lightning Lane and Universal Express, and decide whether they are worth it for your dates.
  • Use the official apps for wait times, maps and mobile food ordering.
  • Book popular dining and character meals well ahead.

When to Visit

Crowds, prices and weather all vary through the year. Late January to early March (outside holiday weekends) and late April to May are often good balances of manageable crowds and pleasant weather. Summer (June–August) is hot, humid and busy with school holidays, with afternoon thunderstorms almost daily; this is also the start of hurricane season, which officially runs from June to November. Autumn brings Halloween and food-and-wine events, and Christmas to New Year is among the busiest periods of the year.

The Theme Parks

Magic Kingdom is the classic Disney park with Cinderella Castle, parades and fireworks; it is the best choice for young children. EPCOT combines futuristic rides with World Showcase, where pavilions represent different countries' food and culture. Hollywood Studios focuses on films, with Star Wars: Galaxy's Edge and Toy Story Land. Animal Kingdom mixes a safari ride with the Pandora area inspired by Avatar.

At Universal, the Wizarding World of Harry Potter is split between two parks, connected by the Hogwarts Express (which requires a park-to-park ticket). Epic Universe added new themed worlds. SeaWorld Orlando offers roller coasters alongside marine-life exhibits, and LEGOLAND Florida in nearby Winter Haven suits younger children.

Beyond the Parks

  • Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex on the Space Coast, about an hour's drive east, displays the Space Shuttle Atlantis and real rockets; you may even be able to watch a launch from the area.
  • Natural springs such as Wekiwa Springs and Blue Spring State Park (where manatees gather in winter) offer swimming, kayaking and wildlife.
  • Winter Park, a charming town with a scenic boat tour through lakes and canals.
  • Disney Springs and Universal CityWalk for free-to-enter shopping, dining and entertainment.
  • Airboat rides through wetlands to spot alligators.
  • Beaches on both Florida coasts are within day-trip range.

Eating Out

Theme-park food ranges from quick-service burgers to themed fine dining. Outside the parks, International Drive and Dr Phillips' "Restaurant Row" have chain and upscale restaurants, while Mills 50 is known for Vietnamese and other Asian food. Florida specialities include Key lime pie, Cuban sandwiches and fresh Gulf seafood. Vegetarian, vegan and allergy-friendly options are widely available, especially in Disney parks, where chefs can often accommodate requests.

Getting There and Around

Orlando International Airport (MCO) is one of the busiest in the US, with many domestic and international flights. Orlando Sanford (SFB) serves some low-cost and charter carriers. Brightline high-speed trains link MCO with Miami and Fort Lauderdale.

A rental car gives the most flexibility, but factor in parking fees at the parks and hotels. Disney runs free buses, monorails, boats and the Skyliner gondola between its resorts and parks for resort guests. Universal hotels offer shuttles or walking paths. Ride-hailing apps are widely used, and I-Ride Trolley runs along International Drive.

Where to Stay

  • Disney resort hotels: Free transport and early park entry benefits.
  • Universal on-site hotels: Walkable or shuttles; some include Express passes.
  • International Drive: Central, with many hotels and restaurants.
  • Lake Buena Vista and Kissimmee: Good-value hotels and vacation rentals with pools.

Budget Tips

  • Visit midweek in quieter months and buy multi-day tickets.
  • Rent a vacation home with a kitchen for families.
  • Bring refillable water bottles; parks provide free water at many counters.
  • Skip paid queue passes on quieter days.

Practical Information

  • Entry: Visitors from Visa Waiver Program countries need an ESTA; others need a US visa. Check official US government sites.
  • Currency: US dollar (USD). Tipping of around 15–20% is customary in sit-down restaurants.
  • Plugs: Types A and B, 120V.

Best time to visit

Weather and prices in Orlando vary by season. Shoulder seasons (just before or after the peak) often balance good weather with lower fares — use flexible dates in the search and compare a few weeks.
