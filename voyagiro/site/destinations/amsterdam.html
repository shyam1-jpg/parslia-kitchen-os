Amsterdam Travel Guide

Overview

Amsterdam was built on water and trade. Its seventeenth-century Golden Age left behind a horseshoe of canals lined with tall, narrow merchants' houses, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Today it is one of Europe's most liveable capitals: compact, bike-mad, liberal and international, with world-class art museums alongside cosy "brown cafés", flower markets and houseboats. It is easy to see the highlights in a few days, but the city rewards those who slow down, explore the quieter canals of the Jordaan and take a day trip into the Dutch countryside.

Best Time to Visit

  • Spring (April–May): Tulip season. The Keukenhof gardens open for a few weeks and the bulb fields around Lisse bloom. King's Day on 27 April turns the city orange with street parties and flea markets. Expect high demand for rooms.
  • Summer (June–August): Long, mild days, canal-side terraces and festivals. It is busy and accommodation is expensive.
  • Autumn (September–November): Fewer visitors, cultural events, and some rainy days; often better value.
  • Winter (December–February): Cold and damp, but the Amsterdam Light Festival illuminates the canals, and museums are quieter.

Top Attractions

  1. Canal Ring – Walk or take a boat along the Herengracht, Keizersgracht and Prinsengracht; the "Nine Streets" area is full of small shops.
  2. Rijksmuseum – The national museum, home to Rembrandt's The Night Watch and Vermeer's The Milkmaid.
  3. Anne Frank House – The hiding place of Anne Frank and her family during World War II. Tickets are released online only and sell out quickly, so book as soon as possible.
  4. Van Gogh Museum – The world's largest collection of Van Gogh's work; timed tickets required.
  5. Jordaan – A picturesque neighbourhood of narrow canals, markets such as the Noordermarkt, and traditional cafés.
  6. Vondelpark – The city's favourite park for cycling, picnics and people-watching.
  7. A'DAM Lookout and Amsterdam Noord – Take the free ferry behind Central Station to a creative former shipyard district with views back over the city.
  8. Day trips – Zaanse Schans windmills, the fishing villages of Volendam and Marken, Haarlem, or the Keukenhof in spring.

Food to Try

Dutch food is hearty and snack-driven. Try stroopwafels fresh from a market stall, raw herring with onions from a herring cart, bitterballen with mustard in a café, and Dutch apple pie with whipped cream. Poffertjes (mini pancakes) and full-size pannenkoeken are popular with families. In winter, stamppot (mashed potatoes with vegetables) is classic comfort food. Amsterdam is also known for Indonesian rijsttafel, a feast of many small dishes reflecting colonial history. Vegetarian and vegan options are abundant, and Surinamese roti shops are excellent value. Foodhallen in Oud-West is good for sampling.

Getting There and Getting Around

Amsterdam Schiphol (AMS) is one of Europe's major hubs, with direct trains to Amsterdam Centraal in about 15–20 minutes. International trains arrive from Paris, Brussels, London (Eurostar) and Germany.

The city is best explored by bike, on foot, by tram and by metro. GVB trams and buses accept contactless bank cards (tap in and out). If you cycle, follow traffic rules, use bike lanes, and lock your bike to a fixed rack. As a pedestrian, never walk in the red bike lanes. Canal boats are a scenic way to sightsee.

Where to Stay

  • Canal Ring (Grachtengordel): Classic views and central, but expensive.
  • Jordaan: Charming and residential, close to the Anne Frank House.
  • Museum Quarter and Oud-Zuid: Elegant, quiet and near the major museums and Vondelpark.
  • De Pijp: Lively, with the Albert Cuyp market and diverse restaurants.
  • Amsterdam Noord and Oost: Creative, newer areas, often better value with quick ferry or metro links.
  • Haarlem or Zaandam: Nearby towns with cheaper hotels and short train rides.

Budget Tips

  • Consider staying outside the centre or in nearby towns and commuting by train.
  • Take the free ferries across the IJ to Noord.
  • Buy a museum card if you plan many museum visits, and book timed tickets in advance.
  • Picnic in Vondelpark with supermarket supplies.
  • Use public transport day tickets if you will take many trips.
  • Hotel prices are typically lower midweek and outside tulip season and summer.

Practical Information

  • Currency: Euro (EUR). Many places are card-only, and some do not accept credit cards, so a debit card helps.
  • Language: Dutch; almost everyone speaks excellent English.
