Doha Travel Guide

Overview

Doha has transformed itself into one of the Gulf's most polished cities, yet it still feels calmer and more compact than many of its neighbours. Qatar's capital curves around a crescent-shaped bay, with a futuristic skyline on one side and a restored traditional souq and world-class museums on the other. Many travellers first meet Doha on a stopover at Hamad International Airport, but it deserves a few days of its own, especially in the cooler months.

Best Time to Visit

  • November to March: The most pleasant period, with warm days and cooler evenings ideal for walking the Corniche, desert trips and outdoor dining. This is peak season for events and hotel demand.
  • April and October: Shoulder months, increasingly hot but still manageable early and late in the day.
  • May to September: Very hot and often humid. Life moves indoors to air-conditioned malls, museums and hotels, and rates are frequently lower.
  • Ramadan: Eating and drinking in public during daylight hours is not permitted. Opening times change, and evenings come alive with iftar gatherings. Check the dates before travelling.

Top Attractions

  1. Museum of Islamic Art – Designed by I. M. Pei, it holds an outstanding collection spanning centuries and continents. The surrounding MIA Park offers some of the best skyline views.
  2. National Museum of Qatar – Its architecture, inspired by the desert rose crystal, is as striking as its displays on the country's history and people.
  3. Souq Waqif – A restored traditional market of spices, textiles, perfumes and falcons, with restaurants and shisha cafés that come alive in the evening. The nearby Falcon Souq and Falcon Hospital are unique.
  4. The Corniche – A long waterfront promenade around Doha Bay, perfect at sunset. Traditional dhow boat trips leave from here.
  5. Katara Cultural Village – Galleries, an amphitheatre, mosques, a beach and restaurants.
  6. The Pearl-Qatar and Msheireb Downtown – An artificial island with marinas and shops, and a regenerated district of sustainable architecture with heritage house museums.
  7. 3-2-1 Qatar Olympic and Sports Museum – An interactive museum near Khalifa International Stadium.
  8. Desert and Inland Sea – A 4x4 trip to Khor Al Adaid, where dunes meet the sea, often includes dune bashing and sandboarding. Go with a licensed operator.

Food

Qatari and Gulf cuisine features machboos (spiced rice with meat or fish), harees (wheat and meat porridge, popular in Ramadan), balaleet (sweet vermicelli with egg) and luqaimat (sweet fried dumplings with date syrup). Souq Waqif is the best place to sample local and Middle Eastern dishes. Thanks to a large South Asian population, Doha has an excellent range of Indian, Pakistani and Sri Lankan restaurants, and vegetarian food is easy to find. Karak tea, a spiced milky tea, is the local favourite.

Getting There and Getting Around

Hamad International Airport (DOH) is a major global hub with connections across Europe, Asia, Africa and the Americas. The Doha Metro's Red Line links the airport with the city centre.

The Doha Metro is modern, clean and covers key areas including Souq Waqif, Msheireb, Katara and Lusail, with a tram network in parts of the city. Taxis and ride-hailing apps are widely available. Walking is pleasant in the cooler months, especially along the Corniche, but distances can be large.

Where to Stay

  • West Bay: Skyscrapers, big international hotels and beach clubs.
  • Msheireb and Souq Waqif area: Close to the old town, museums and the metro.
  • The Pearl: Marina-side apartments and hotels.
  • Lusail: A newer city north of Doha with modern hotels and leisure areas.
  • Near the airport: Convenient for short stopovers.

Budget Tips

  • The Corniche, Souq Waqif, Katara and MIA Park are free to explore.
  • Use the metro rather than taxis for longer journeys.
  • Eat at casual Indian and Middle Eastern restaurants rather than hotel dining.
  • Visit in the hotter months for lower hotel rates, planning indoor activities for midday.
  • On a long layover, check whether airline stopover packages or hotel deals are available.

Practical and Visa Information

  • Visa: Entry rules change and depend on your passport. Use the destination country's official government site and your own government's travel advice. This guide does not say who can enter.
  • Currency: Qatari riyal (QAR). Cards are widely accepted.
  • Language: Arabic, with English widely spoken.
  • Customs and dress: Dress modestly in public places, covering shoulders and knees. Public displays of affection are frowned upon, and alcohol is only served in licensed venues.
  • Heat: Carry water and sun protection.
  • Plugs: Type G.
