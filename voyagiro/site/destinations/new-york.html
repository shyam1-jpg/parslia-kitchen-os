New York City Travel Guide

New York is less a single destination than five boroughs stitched together by bridges, ferries and one of the oldest subway systems in the world. Manhattan delivers the postcard views, from the Empire State Building to the Brooklyn Bridge, but much of the city's character lives in Brooklyn's brownstone streets, Queens' extraordinary food scene and the waterfronts that have been reclaimed as parks. First visits tend to be busy; the trick is to group sights by neighbourhood and leave time to simply walk.

The Seasons

Spring (April–June): Central Park blooms, street fairs begin and temperatures are comfortable for walking. Late spring is popular, so book rooms early.

Summer (July–August): Hot and often humid, with free outdoor concerts, rooftop bars and beach trips to Coney Island or the Rockaways. Some locals leave town, and hotels can be slightly cheaper than in autumn.

Autumn (September–November): Many consider this the best time. Crisp air, autumn colour in the parks and a packed cultural calendar. The Thanksgiving Day Parade marks the start of the holiday season.

Winter (December–March): December is magical and expensive, with the Rockefeller Center tree, window displays and skating rinks. January to early March is cold, sometimes snowy, and usually the cheapest time for hotels.

Highlights Worth Your Time

  1. Central Park – Rent a bike, row a boat on the lake, or walk from Bethesda Terrace to the Bow Bridge and Strawberry Fields.
  2. Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island – Book official ferry tickets in advance. For a free alternative view, ride the Staten Island Ferry.
  3. Broadway and Times Square – See a show in the Theater District; Times Square itself is best experienced briefly at night.
  4. The Metropolitan Museum of Art – Five thousand years of art on Fifth Avenue. Pick a few wings and come back if you can.
  5. Brooklyn Bridge and DUMBO – Walk across from Manhattan in the early morning, then explore DUMBO's cobbled streets and Brooklyn Bridge Park.
  6. The High Line – An elevated park built on an old railway, running through Chelsea to Hudson Yards.
  7. 9/11 Memorial & Museum – A moving tribute at the site of the World Trade Center; the reflecting pools are free to visit.
  8. Observation decks – Choose between the Empire State Building, Top of the Rock, One World Observatory, Edge or SUMMIT for skyline views.

What to Eat

New York is a city of iconic cheap eats as much as fine dining. Fold a slice of New York-style pizza, eat a bagel with cream cheese and lox, and try pastrami on rye at a traditional Jewish deli. Street carts sell hot dogs, pretzels and halal chicken over rice. Cheesecake is a must. Head to Flushing in Queens for Chinese and Korean food, Jackson Heights for Indian, Nepali and Tibetan dishes, Arthur Avenue in the Bronx for Italian, and Chinatown or Koreatown in Manhattan. Food halls like Chelsea Market and weekend markets such as Smorgasburg are great for sampling.

Arriving and Getting Around

Three major airports serve the city. JFK handles most long-haul international flights and connects to the subway via AirTrain. Newark (EWR), in New Jersey, has AirTrain links to NJ Transit and Amtrak trains into Penn Station. LaGuardia (LGA) mainly serves domestic routes and is reached by bus or taxi. Amtrak trains arrive at Moynihan Train Hall/Penn Station from Washington, Boston and beyond.

The subway runs 24 hours and reaches almost everywhere; tap a contactless card or phone with OMNY, and a weekly fare cap applies. Buses fill the gaps, and the NYC Ferry is a scenic way to reach Brooklyn and Queens waterfronts. Yellow cabs and ride-hailing apps are plentiful, but traffic is heavy. Manhattan's street grid makes walking easy above 14th Street.

Where to Base Yourself

  • Midtown: Close to Times Square, Broadway and many sights. Convenient for first-timers, though busy and often pricey.
  • Lower Manhattan and the Financial District: Near the 9/11 Memorial and ferries; hotels can be cheaper on weekends.
  • Greenwich Village, SoHo and the Lower East Side: Characterful streets, restaurants and nightlife.
  • Upper West Side: Residential and calmer, next to Central Park and the American Museum of Natural History, good for families.
  • Brooklyn (Williamsburg, DUMBO, Downtown Brooklyn): Trendy, with skyline views and often better value.
  • Long Island City, Queens: Quick subway ride to Midtown, usually cheaper hotels.

Ways to Save

  • Many experiences are free: Central Park, the High Line, the Staten Island Ferry, walking the Brooklyn Bridge and the 9/11 Memorial pools.
  • Some museums offer pay-what-you-wish or free evenings; check their websites.
  • City passes can be worthwhile if you plan several paid attractions.
  • Look for discount theatre tickets at the TKTS booth or digital lotteries.
  • Stay in Queens, Brooklyn or Jersey City near a train line.
  • Remember that prices are shown before sales tax, and tipping in restaurants is customary.
