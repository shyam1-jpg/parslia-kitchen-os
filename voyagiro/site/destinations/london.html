London Travel Guide

Overview

London is two thousand years of history layered under a restless, modern capital. Roman walls sit a few streets from glass towers in the City, royal palaces share parkland with joggers and food trucks, and many of the world's great museums charge nothing to walk in. It is a city of villages: Georgian squares in Bloomsbury, market streets in Shoreditch, riverside pubs in Greenwich and grand Victorian terraces in Kensington each feel distinct. Most visitors come for the landmarks and leave remembering the neighbourhoods, the theatre and the sheer variety of food.

Best Time to Visit

London is a year-round destination, and the weather is rarely extreme.

  • Spring (March–May): Parks fill with blossom, daylight lengthens and crowds are moderate. Showers are common, so pack a light waterproof.
  • Summer (June–August): The busiest and usually warmest season, with outdoor cinema, open-air theatre, festivals and long evenings. Accommodation is at its most expensive and popular sights have longer queues.
  • Autumn (September–November): Often mild in September, with fewer school-holiday crowds. Galleries launch major exhibitions and the theatre season is in full swing.
  • Winter (December–February): Cold, damp and dark by late afternoon, but festive lights, Christmas markets and ice rinks add atmosphere. January and February are generally the cheapest months for hotels outside the Christmas period.

Top Attractions

  1. Tower of London – A medieval fortress on the Thames that houses the Crown Jewels. The Yeoman Warder tours bring its darker history to life.
  2. British Museum – Free to enter, with collections spanning the Rosetta Stone, Egyptian mummies and the Parthenon sculptures. Choose a few galleries rather than trying to see everything.
  3. South Bank and the London Eye – A riverside walk from Westminster Bridge to Tower Bridge passes the London Eye, the Southbank Centre, Tate Modern and Shakespeare's Globe.
  4. Westminster – The Houses of Parliament, Big Ben (Elizabeth Tower) and Westminster Abbey sit within a few minutes' walk of each other.
  5. Buckingham Palace and St James's Park – Watch the Changing of the Guard on scheduled days, then stroll through one of the city's prettiest royal parks.
  6. Borough Market – One of London's oldest food markets, ideal for grazing on cheese, street food and baked goods.
  7. Greenwich – Take a river boat to see the Cutty Sark, the Royal Observatory and the Prime Meridian, with sweeping views from Greenwich Park.
  8. Natural History Museum – Another free museum in South Kensington, loved for its architecture and dinosaur galleries.

Food to Try

London's food scene reflects its global population. Try a traditional Sunday roast in a pub, fish and chips from a proper chippy, and a full English breakfast. Afternoon tea with scones, clotted cream and jam is a classic treat. Beyond British staples, Brick Lane and Whitechapel are known for Bangladeshi curry houses, Southall and Wembley for Indian and Punjabi food, Chinatown in Soho for dim sum and roast duck, and Dalston and Peckham for Turkish and West African cooking. Markets such as Borough, Maltby Street and Camden are good for cheap, varied eating.

Getting There and Getting Around

London is served by several airports: Heathrow and Gatwick handle most long-haul flights, while Stansted, Luton and London City serve many short-haul and low-cost routes. Heathrow connects to central London by the Elizabeth line, the Heathrow Express and the Piccadilly line. Gatwick has fast trains to Victoria and London Bridge. Eurostar trains arrive at St Pancras from Paris, Brussels and Amsterdam.

Within the city, the Underground (the Tube), Overground, Elizabeth line, buses and river boats are all covered by contactless bank cards or an Oyster card, with daily fare caps that stop you overpaying. Buses are often cheaper than the Tube and great for sightseeing from the top deck. Central London is very walkable, and Santander Cycles are available for short hops.

Where to Stay

  • Westminster and the West End: Walking distance to theatres, Covent Garden and major sights. Convenient but usually expensive.
  • South Kensington and Kensington: Elegant streets near the free museums and Hyde Park. Good for families.
  • Bloomsbury and King's Cross: Central, well connected and close to the British Museum and Eurostar. A strong mid-range choice.
  • Shoreditch and the East End: Creative, lively nightlife, street art and markets. Popular with younger travellers.
  • South Bank and Southwark: Riverside location near Borough Market and Tate Modern.
  • Outer zones (e.g. Stratford, Earl's Court, Paddington outskirts): Often better value, with fast Tube links into the centre.

Budget Tips

  • Make the most of free museums: the British Museum, National Gallery, Tate Modern, Natural History Museum, Science Museum and V&A.
  • Use contactless payment so daily fare caps apply automatically.
  • Book attractions such as the Tower of London and the London Eye online in advance, where discounts are often offered.
  • Look for day-of or rush theatre tickets and discount booths in Leicester Square.
  • Eat your main meal at lunchtime, when many restaurants offer set menus, and use supermarket meal deals for quick lunches.
  • Flights and hotels are often cheaper midweek and outside school holidays.
